## Summary

...gravitates to the edge of any scrum, shoves, butts, and squirms into the heart of the crowd. Soon he is within arm's length of the girl, blocked by an enormous prole and an almost equally enormous woman, probably his wife — an impenetrable wall of flesh. Winston wriggles sideways and with a violent lunge drives his shoulder between them. For a moment his guts feel ground to pulp between two muscular hips; then he is through, sweating. He is next to her, shoulder to shoulder, both staring fixedly ahead. A long line of trucks passes, wooden-faced guards with sub-machine guns at each corner. Inside, small men in shabby greenish uniforms squat jammed together. Their sad faces gaze out, utterly incurious. When a truck jolts there is a clank of metal: all wear leg-irons. Load after load of sad faces goes by. Winston knows they are there but sees them only in snatches. The girl's shoulder and arm to the elbow press his; her cheek is almost warm enough to feel. She has taken charge, as in the canteen. She speaks in the same expressionless murmur, lips barely moving, easily drowned by voices and rumbling trucks. Can he hear her? Yes. Can he get Sunday afternoon off? Yes. Then listen carefully — he will have to remember this. Go to Paddington Station. With a military precision that astonishes him she outlines the route: a half-hour train; left outside the station; two kilometres along the road; a gate with the top bar missing; a path across a field; a grass-grown lane; a track between bushes; a dead tree with moss on it.
