## Summary

...were a little under control the terror returned. Sometimes with a fading hope he thinks of O'Brien and the razor blade. It is thinkable that the razor blade might arrive concealed in his food, if he were ever fed. More dimly he thinks of Julia. Somewhere or other she is suffering, perhaps far worse than he. She might be screaming with pain at this moment. He thinks: if he could save Julia by doubling his own pain, would he do it? Yes, he would. But that is merely an intellectual decision, taken because he knows that he ought to take it. He does not feel it. In this place you could not feel anything, except pain and foreknowledge of pain. Besides, was it possible, when you were actually suffering it, to wish for any reason that your own pain should increase? But that question is not answerable yet. The boots are approaching again. The door opens. O'Brien comes in. Winston starts to his feet. The shock of the sight has driven all caution out of him. For the first time in many years he forgets the presence of the telescreen. "They've got you too!" he cries. "They got me a long time ago," says O'Brien with a mild, almost regretful irony. He steps aside. From behind him there emerges a broad-chested guard with a long black truncheon in his hand. "You know this, Winston," says O'Brien. "Don't deceive yourself. You did know it — you have always known it." Yes, he sees now, he has always known it. But there is no time to think of that. All he has eyes for is the truncheon in the guard's hand. It might fall anywhere: on the crown, on the tip of the ear, on the upper arm, on the elbow —— The elbow! He has slumped to his knees, almost paralysed, clasping the stricken elbow with his other hand. Everything has exploded into yellow light. Inconceivable, inconceivable that one blow could cause such pain! The light clears and he can see the other two looking down at him. The guard is laughing at his contortions. One question at any rate is answered. Never, for any reason on earth, could you wish for an increase of pain. Of pain you could wish only one thing: that it should stop.
