## Summary

...glimpse of the girl at a far table with two others. She seems not to have seen him; he does not look again. The afternoon is more bearable. A delicate, difficult job arrives: falsifying two-year-old production reports so as to discredit an Inner Party man now "under a cloud." This is work Winston is good at, and for more than two hours he shuts her out. Then her face returns, and with it a raging need to be alone. Until he is alone he cannot think the new fact through. Tonight is a Community Centre night. He wolfs a tasteless meal, hurries to the Centre, sits through a solemn discussion group, plays two games of table tennis, drinks several gins, and lasts half an hour of a lecture called "Ingsoc in relation to chess." His soul writhes with boredom, but for once he has no impulse to shirk. At "I LOVE YOU" the wish to stay alive welled up; minor risks suddenly seem stupid. Not until twenty-three hours, home in bed — dark, safe from the telescreen if you keep silent — can he think continuously. The problem is physical: how to contact her and arrange a meeting. He no longer thinks she is laying a trap. Her unmistakable agitation when she passed the note proved she was frightened out of her wits. Refusing her does not even cross his mind. Only five nights ago he thought of smashing her skull; that no longer matters. He thinks of her naked youthful body from the dream.
