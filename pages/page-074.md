## Summary

...stratum, wrecking a geological theory. Published and explained, it would have been enough to blow the Party to atoms. He went straight on working. As soon as he saw what the photograph meant, he covered it with another sheet. Luckily it had unrolled upside-down to the telescreen. He put his pad on his knee and pushed back to get as far from the screen as he could. A blank face and even breathing can be controlled; the heartbeat cannot, and the telescreen is delicate enough to pick it up. He waits what he judges to be ten minutes, terrified that a draught across the desk will betray him. Then, without uncovering it, he drops the photograph into the memory hole with other waste. Within a minute it is probably ash. That was ten or eleven years ago. Today he would probably have kept it. Oddly, having held it still feels like it matters, even though photo and event exist only as memory. Is the Party's hold on the past weaker because a piece of evidence that no longer exists once did? Today, even if the photo could be resurrected, it might not be evidence. By then Oceania was no longer at war with Eurasia; the three men would have "betrayed" Eastasia instead. There have been more changes since. The confessions have likely been rewritten until original facts and dates mean nothing. The past not only changes — it changes continuously. What most gives him a nightmare sense is that he has never clearly understood why the huge imposture is undertaken. The immediate advantages of falsifying the past...
