## Summary

Syme asks if Winston saw yesterday's hanging. Winston says he was working and will watch it on film. Syme calls that a poor substitute; his eyes seem to say he knows why Winston stayed away. Intellectually Syme is venomously orthodox: he gloats over helicopter raids, confessions, and executions in the Ministry of Love cellars. Winston tries to steer him onto Newspeak, where he is expert and interesting, and turns aside to escape those staring eyes. Syme recalls the hanging with pleasure — he likes to see prisoners kick, and especially the bright-blue tongue at the end. A prole server yells for the next customer. Onto each tray goes the regulation lunch: pink-grey stew in a metal pannikin, bread, a cheese cube, milkless Victory Coffee, and one saccharine tablet. They buy gin in handleless mugs, pick a table under a telescreen, and unpack beside a pool of stew that looks like vomit. Winston steels himself, gulps the oily gin, then finds he is hungry and eats the sloppy stew with its spongy pink "meat." They do not speak again until the pannikins are empty.
