## Summary

Not with those swine, she says — but plenty would if they got half a chance; they are not so holy as they make out. His heart leaps. Scores of times: he wishes it had been hundreds — thousands. Anything that hints at corruption fills him with wild hope. Who knows? Perhaps the Party is rotten under the surface, its cult of hard work and self-denial only a sham hiding iniquity. If he could have infected the lot of them with disease, how gladly he would have done so — anything to rot, weaken, undermine. He pulls her down so they kneel face to face. The more men she has had, the more he loves her — does she understand? Yes, perfectly. He hates purity, hates goodness; he wants no virtue anywhere; he wants everyone corrupt to the bones. Then she ought to suit him: she is corrupt to the bones. Does she like doing this — not simply him, the thing in itself? She adores it. That is above all what he wanted to hear. Not merely love of one person but the animal instinct, simple undifferentiated desire: that, he thinks, is the force that would tear the Party to pieces. He presses her down on the grass among the fallen bluebells. This time there is no difficulty. Presently their breathing slows and in a pleasant helplessness they fall apart. The sun seems hotter. Both are sleepy. He pulls the discarded overalls partly over her. Almost at once they sleep for about half an hour. Winston wakes first and watches the freckled face, still peacefully asleep, pillowed on her palm. Except for her mouth you could not call her beautiful. There is a line or two round the eyes if you look closely. The short dark hair is extraordinarily thick and soft. He realises he still does not know her surname or where she lives.
