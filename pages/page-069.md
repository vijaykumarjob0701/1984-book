## Summary

...lived in by rich men with as many as thirty servants. These "capitalists" are pictured as fat, ugly, and wicked, in frock coats and shiny stovepipe top hats that nobody else may wear. They owned all land, houses, factories, and money. Disobey them and they could jail you or take your job and starve you. Ordinary people had to cringe, bow, take off their caps, and say "Sir." The chief capitalist was the King. Winston already knows the rest of the list: bishops in lawn sleeves, judges in ermine, the pillory, stocks, treadmill, cat-o'-nine-tails, the Lord Mayor's Banquet, kissing the Pope's toe. There is also jus primae noctis — probably left out of a children's book — the claimed "law" that every capitalist could sleep with any woman in his factories. How much of this is lies? It might be true that the average person is better off now. The only contrary evidence is the mute protest in your bones, the feeling that life is intolerable and must once have been different. What seems most typical of modern life is not cruelty so much as bareness, dinginess, and listlessness. Life matches neither telescreen lies nor even the Party's own ideal. Huge areas of it, even for a Party member, are neutral: just slogging...

| stocks | a similar frame that locked the feet |
| treadmill | a machine prisoners were forced to walk to punish them |
| mute | silent; without words |
| characteristic | typical; the most defining quality |
| bareness | emptiness; having almost nothing |
| dinginess | dirty dullness |
| listlessness | having no energy or will |
| resemblance | looking like something else |
| slogging | working slowly through something dull and hard |
