## Summary

...to bring in the prisoner they are leading. The poet Ampleforth shambles into the cell. The door clangs shut again. Ampleforth makes one or two uncertain movements from side to side, as though thinking there is another door to go out of, then begins to wander up and down. He has not yet noticed Winston. His troubled eyes gaze at the wall about a metre above Winston's head. He is shoeless; large dirty toes stick out of the holes in his socks. He is also several days away from a shave. A scrubby beard covers his face to the cheekbones, giving him an air of ruffianism that goes oddly with his large weak frame and nervous movements. Winston rouses himself a little from his lethargy. He must speak to Ampleforth, and risk the yell from the telescreen. It is even conceivable that Ampleforth is the bearer of the razor blade. "Ampleforth," he says. There is no yell. Ampleforth pauses, mildly startled. His eyes focus slowly on Winston. "Ah, Smith! You too!" What is he in for? To tell the truth — he sits down awkwardly opposite Winston — there is only one offence, is there not? And has he committed it? Apparently he has. He presses his temples, as though trying to remember. These things happen. He has been able to recall one instance — a possible instance. It was an indiscretion, undoubtedly. They were producing a definitive edition of the poems of Kipling. He allowed the word "God" to remain at the end of a line. He could not help it! It was impossible to change the line. The rhyme was "rod." Does Winston realize there are only twelve rhymes to "rod" in the entire language? For days he had racked his brains. There *was* no other rhyme.
