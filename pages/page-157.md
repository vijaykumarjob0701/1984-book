## Summary

...looked almost black, but in the decanter it gleamed like a ruby. It has a sour-sweet smell. Julia picks up her glass and sniffs it with frank curiosity. "It is called wine," says O'Brien with a faint smile. They will have read about it in books, no doubt. Not much of it gets to the Outer Party. His face grows solemn again, and he raises his glass: it is fitting they should begin by drinking a health. To their Leader: To Emmanuel Goldstein. Winston takes up his glass with a certain eagerness. Wine is a thing he has read and dreamed about. Like the glass paperweight or Charrington's half-remembered rhymes, it belongs to the vanished, romantic past — the olden time, as he likes to call it in secret thoughts. For some reason he has always thought of wine as intensely sweet, like blackberry jam, with an immediate intoxicating effect. Actually, when he comes to swallow it, the stuff is distinctly disappointing. After years of gin-drinking he can barely taste it. He sets down the empty glass. Then there is such a person as Goldstein? Yes, there is such a person, and he is alive. Where, O'Brien does not know. And the conspiracy — the organisation? Is it real? Not simply an invention of the Thought Police? No, it is real. The Brotherhood, they call it. Winston will never learn much more about the Brotherhood than that it exists and that he belongs to it. O'Brien will come back to that presently. He looks at his wrist-watch. It is unwise even for members of the Inner Party to turn off the telescreen for more than half an hour. They ought not to have come here together, and they will have to leave separately. Julia will leave first. They have about twenty minutes. Winston will understand that O'Brien must start by asking certain questions. In general terms, what are they prepared to do? "Anything that we are capable of," says Winston. O'Brien has turned himself a little in his chair so that he is facing Winston. He almost ignores Julia, seeming to take it for granted that Winston can speak for her.
