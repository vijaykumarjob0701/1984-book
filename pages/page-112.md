## Summary

Black market, she says indifferently. She is that sort of girl to look at: good at games, a troop-leader in the Spies, three evenings a week of Junior Anti-Sex League work, hours pasting their rot over London, always one end of a banner, always cheerful, never shirking. Always yell with the crowd — the only way to be safe. The first fragment of chocolate melts on Winston's tongue; the taste is delightful. A memory still moves at the edge of consciousness, strongly felt but not reducible to shape, like something seen from the corner of the eye. He pushes it away, aware only that it is some action he would like to undo and cannot. She is very young, he says — ten or fifteen years younger. What could she see to attract her in a man like him? Something in his face; she thought she would take a chance. She is good at spotting people who don't belong. As soon as she saw him she knew he was against THEM. THEM means the Party, especially the Inner Party, of whom she talks with an open jeering hatred that makes him uneasy, though they are as safe here as anywhere. What astonishes him is the coarseness of her language. Party members are not supposed to swear; he himself seldom does, aloud. Julia seems unable to mention the Party, especially the Inner Party, without the kind of words chalked in dripping alley-ways. He does not dislike it. It is a symptom of her revolt, natural and healthy, like a horse sneezing at bad hay.
