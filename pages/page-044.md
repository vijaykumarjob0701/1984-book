## Summary

Then the chosen lie will enter the permanent records and become truth. Winston does not know why Withers fell. It might be corruption, incompetence, popularity that threatened Big Brother, suspected heresy — or, likeliest, because purges are simply how government works. The phrase "refs unpersons" is the real clue: Withers is already dead. Arrest does not always mean immediate death; some people are freed for a year or two, and very rarely a person you thought long dead reappears at a trial, names hundreds of others, then vanishes for good. Withers, though, is already an unperson: he does not exist and never did. Winston decides not merely to reverse the speech's praise but to change the subject entirely. A standard attack on traitors would be too obvious; a fake front-line victory or production triumph would tangle other records. He needs pure fantasy. Ready-made, the figure of Comrade Ogilvy appears — a man who has just died heroically in battle. Sometimes Big Brother's Order praises a humble ordinary member as a model. Today it will commemorate Ogilvy. No such person exists, but a few lines and two faked photographs will create him. Winston pulls the speakwrite over and begins dictating in Big Brother's familiar manner.
