## Summary

His mother was quite ready to give him more than his share. She took it for granted that "the boy" should have the biggest portion; but however much she gave him he demanded more. At every meal she would beseech him not to be selfish and to remember that his little sister was sick and also needed food, but it was no use. He would cry out with rage when she stopped ladling, try to wrench the saucepan and spoon out of her hands, grab bits from his sister's plate. He knew he was starving the other two, but he could not help it; he even felt he had a right to do it. The clamorous hunger in his belly seemed to justify him. Between meals, if his mother did not stand guard, he was constantly pilfering the wretched store of food on the shelf. One day a chocolate ration was issued — the first for weeks or months. He remembers that precious two-ounce slab (they still talked about ounces then) meant to be divided three ways. Suddenly, as though listening to somebody else, he heard himself demanding in a loud booming voice that he should be given the whole piece. His mother told him not to be greedy. There was a long nagging argument of shouts, whines, tears, remonstrances, bargainings. His tiny sister, clinging to her mother with both hands like a baby monkey, sat looking over her shoulder with large mournful eyes. In the end his mother broke off three-quarters for Winston and gave the other quarter to his sister. The little girl took it and looked at it dully, perhaps not knowing what it was. Winston stood watching her for a moment. Then with a sudden swift spring he snatched the piece out of her hand and fled for the door. "Winston, Winston!" his mother called. "Come back! Give your sister back her chocolate!" He stopped, but did not come back. His mother's anxious eyes were fixed on his face.
