## Summary

"You are the dead," the iron voice repeats. "It was behind the picture," Julia breathes. "It was behind the picture," says the voice. Remain exactly where you are. Make no movement until you are ordered. It is starting, it is starting at last! They can do nothing except stand gazing into one another's eyes. To run for life, to get out of the house before it is too late — no such thought occurs to them. Unthinkable to disobey the iron voice from the wall. There is a snap as though a catch has been turned back, and a crash of breaking glass. The picture has fallen to the floor, uncovering the telescreen behind it. "Now they can see us," says Julia. "Now we can see you," says the voice. Stand out in the middle of the room. Stand back to back. Clasp your hands behind your heads. Do not touch one another. They are not touching, but it seems to him that he can feel Julia's body shaking. Or perhaps it is merely the shaking of his own. He can just stop his teeth from chattering, but his knees are beyond his control. There is a sound of trampling boots below, inside the house and outside. The yard seems full of men. Something is being dragged across the stones. The woman's singing has stopped abruptly. There is a long, rolling clang, as though the washtub has been flung across the yard, and then a confusion of angry shouts which ends in a yell of pain. "The house is surrounded," says Winston. "The house is surrounded," says the voice. He hears Julia snap her teeth together. She supposes they may as well say good-bye. "You may as well say good-bye," says the voice. And then another quite different voice, a thin, cultivated voice which Winston has the impression of having heard before, strikes in. While they are on the subject, it finishes the old St Clement's rhyme: the candle to bed, and the chopper for the head. Something crashes onto the bed behind Winston's back.
