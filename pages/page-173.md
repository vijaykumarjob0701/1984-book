They add nothing to the world's wealth, because whatever they produce is used for war, and the object of waging a war is always to be in a better position to wage another war. Their labour lets the tempo of continuous warfare be speeded up. But if they did not exist, the structure of world society, and the process that maintains it, would not be essentially different. The primary aim of modern warfare — recognized and not recognized at once, by doublethink, in the Inner Party's directing brains — is to use up the products of the machine without raising the general standard of living. Ever since the end of the nineteenth century, the problem of what to do with a surplus of consumption goods has been latent in industrial society. At present, when few people even have enough to eat, the problem is not urgent, and it might not have become so even without artificial destruction. Today's world is bare, hungry, and dilapidated compared with the world before 1914, and still more so compared with the imaginary future people of that period expected. In the early twentieth century, the vision of a future unbelievably rich, leisured, orderly, and efficient — a glittering antiseptic world of glass, steel, and snow-white concrete — was part of nearly every literate person's consciousness. Science and technology were developing at a prodigious speed, and it seemed natural to assume they would go on. That failed to happen, partly because of impoverishment from a long series of wars and revolutions, partly because scientific progress depended on the empirical habit of thought, which could not survive in a strictly regimented society. ## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| tempo | the speed at which something keeps going |
| surplus | extra goods left over after need is met |
| latent | present but not yet urgent |
| dilapidated | worn-out and falling apart |
| leisured | having free time because work is light |
| antiseptic | clean and hospital-bright |
| prodigious | amazingly great |
| impoverishment | being made poor |
| empirical | based on testing and evidence, not on official doctrine |
| regimented | tightly controlled by rules |
| espionage | spying |
