## Summary

Chapter 2 opens with Winston lying on something that feels like a camp bed, except that it is higher off the ground and he is fixed down so that he cannot move. Light stronger than usual falls on his face. O'Brien stands at his side, looking down at him intently. At the other side stands a man in a white coat, holding a hypodermic syringe. Even after his eyes are open he takes in his surroundings only gradually. He has the impression of swimming up into this room from some quite different world, a sort of underwater world far beneath it. How long he has been down there he does not know. Since the arrest he has not seen darkness or daylight. Besides, his memories are not continuous. There have been times when consciousness, even the sort one has in sleep, stopped dead and started again after a blank interval. Whether the intervals were days or weeks or only seconds, there is no way of knowing. With that first blow on the elbow the nightmare started. Later he is to realize that all that then happened was merely a preliminary, a routine interrogation to which nearly all prisoners were subjected. There was a long range of crimes — espionage, sabotage, and the like — to which everyone had to confess as a matter of course. The confession was a formality, though the torture was real. How many times he had been beaten, how long the beatings had continued, he could not remember. Always there were five or six men in black uniforms at him simultaneously. Sometimes it was fists, sometimes truncheons, sometimes steel rods, sometimes boots. There were times when he rolled about the floor, as shameless as an animal, writhing this way and that in an endless, hopeless effort to dodge the kicks, and simply inviting more kicks — ribs, belly, elbows, shins, groin, the base of the spine. There were times when it went on and on until the cruel, wicked, unforgivable thing seemed to him not that the guards continued to beat him but that he could not force himself into losing consciousness. There were times when his nerve so forsook him that he began shouting for mercy...
