## Summary

...within range of a telescreen. The smallest thing can give you away: a nervous tic, an anxious look, muttering to yourself — any hint of abnormality, of having something to hide. Wearing an improper expression (looking incredulous at a victory, for example) is itself a punishable offence. Newspeak even has a word for it: facecrime. The girl has turned her back. Perhaps she is not following him; perhaps two days of sitting close is coincidence. His cigarette has gone out; he saves it to finish after work if the tobacco stays in. The next person may be Thought Police, and he may be in the Ministry of Love cellars within three days, but a cigarette end must not be wasted. Syme pockets his word-list. Parsons starts again, chuckling around his pipe: his two children set fire to an old market-woman's skirt because she wrapped sausages in a Big Brother poster. They sneaked up with matches and burned her quite badly. "Keen as mustard," he says — better Spies training than in his day. The latest issue is toy ear trumpets for listening through keyholes; his little girl tried one on the sitting-room door and claimed she heard twice as much. Only a toy, but it gives them the right idea. A piercing telescreen whistle calls them back to work. All three jump up toward the lifts, and the rest of Winston's tobacco falls out of the cigarette.
