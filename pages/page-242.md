## Summary

Winston's appeal to fossils and stars is brushed off. The earth, O'Brien says, is only as old as human awareness; extinct animals were a nineteenth-century invention; the stars are nearby fires that the Party could reach or wipe out. For sailing and eclipses they may *pretend* the earth goes round the sun — a dual astronomy, which is just doublethink. Every answer lands like a club. Winston still knows that "nothing exists outside the mind" is a named error, but he cannot remember the word. O'Brien supplies it with a smile: solipsism. Then he says this is not that. Call it collective solipsism if you like; it is actually the opposite thing.
