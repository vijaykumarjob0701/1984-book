## Summary

"What shall it be this time?" he says, still with the same faint suggestion of irony. To the confusion of the Thought Police? To the death of Big Brother? To humanity? To the future? "To the past," says Winston. "The past is more important," O'Brien agrees gravely. They empty their glasses, and a moment later Julia stands up to go. O'Brien takes a small box from the top of a cabinet and hands her a flat white tablet to place on her tongue. It is important, he says, not to go out smelling of wine: the lift attendants are very observant. As soon as the door has shut behind her he appears to forget her existence. He takes another pace or two up and down, then stops. There are details to be settled. He assumes Winston has a hiding-place of some kind? Winston explains about the room over Mr Charrington's shop. That will do for the moment. Later they will arrange something else. It is important to change one's hiding-place frequently. Meanwhile O'Brien will send a copy of *the book* — even O'Brien, Winston notices, seems to pronounce the words as though they were in italics — Goldstein's book, as soon as possible. It may be some days before he can get hold of one. There are not many in existence. The Thought Police hunt them down and destroy them almost as fast as they can be produced. It makes very little difference. The book is indestructible. If the last copy were gone, they could reproduce it almost word for word. Does Winston carry a brief-case to work? As a rule, yes. What is it like? Black, very shabby. With two straps. Black, two straps, very shabby — good. One day in the fairly near future — O'Brien cannot give a date — one of the messages among Winston's morning's work will contain a misprinted word, and he will have to ask for a repeat.
