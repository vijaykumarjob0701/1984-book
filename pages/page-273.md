## Summary

FREE cannot mean politically or intellectually free, because those ideas no longer exist as namable things. Cutting the word-stock is an end in itself: Newspeak is meant to shrink thought, not grow it. The language is based on English but would already sound barely intelligible. Words fall into three classes: the A vocabulary (everyday), the B vocabulary (compound political words), and the C vocabulary. Grammar is discussed with A, because the same rules cover all three. A-words are for eating, dressing, work, and other ordinary acts — HIT, RUN, DOG, TREE — but far fewer than in real English, and with no shades of meaning. You could not write literature or argue politics in A. Each word is meant to be one blunt sound for one thing or action. The first big grammar fact is almost complete interchangeability of parts of speech.
