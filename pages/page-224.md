## Summary

He does not remember any ending to his interrogation. There was a period of blackness and then the cell, or room, in which he now is had gradually materialized round him. He is almost flat on his back, and unable to move. His body is held down at every essential point. Even the back of his head is gripped in some manner. O'Brien is looking down at him gravely and rather sadly. His face, seen from below, looks coarse and worn, with pouches under the eyes and tired lines from nose to chin. He is older than Winston had thought him; he is perhaps forty-eight or fifty. Under his hand there is a dial with a lever on top and figures running round the face. "I told you," says O'Brien, "that if we met again it would be here." "Yes," says Winston. Without any warning except a slight movement of O'Brien's hand, a wave of pain floods his body. It is a frightening pain, because he cannot see what is happening, and he has the feeling that some mortal injury is being done to him. He does not know whether the thing is really happening, or whether the effect is electrically produced; but his body is being wrenched out of shape, the joints slowly torn apart. Although the pain has brought the sweat out on his forehead, the worst of all is the fear that his backbone is about to snap. He sets his teeth and breathes hard through his nose, trying to keep silent as long as possible. O'Brien, watching his face, names the fear: in another moment something is going to break, and Winston's especial fear is that it will be his backbone. Winston can see the vertebrae snapping apart and the spinal fluid dripping out of them. That is what he is thinking, is it not? Winston does not answer. O'Brien draws back the lever on the dial. The wave of pain recedes almost as quickly as it had come. "That was forty," says O'Brien. The numbers on this dial run up to a hundred. Will Winston please remember, throughout their conversation, that O'Brien has it in his power to inflict pain at any moment and to whatever degree he chooses?
