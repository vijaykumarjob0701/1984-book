## Summary

Chapter 6 opens in the Chestnut Tree Café, almost empty, at the lonely hour of fifteen. Dusty tables, tinny telescreen music, the poster face on the wall. Winston sits in his usual corner. An unasked waiter fills the glass with Victory Gin and clove-saccharine. He is waiting for a possible Ministry of Peace bulletin. The African front is bad: Oceania is at war with Eurasia (and always has been); a Eurasian army is driving south; even Oceanic territory may be threatened for the first time. Excitement flares and dies. He cannot hold one subject. He drains the gin, shudders, almost retches. Worst is that the oily smell is mixed, night and day, with the smell of those — he will not name them, even in thought, and he tries not to picture them.
