## Summary

He says the square is full of telescreens. She says a crowd does not matter. Any signal? No: do not come up until she is among a lot of people, and do not look at her — just stay near. Time: nineteen hours. All right. Ampleforth does not see Winston and sits elsewhere. They do not speak again or, so far as two people at one table can help it, look at each other. She finishes quickly and leaves; he stays to smoke. He is in Victory Square before the hour and wanders round the enormous fluted column, Big Brother's statue gazing south toward the skies where he "vanquished" Eurasian planes (Eastasian, a few years ago) in the Battle of Airstrip One. In the street is a horseman supposed to be Oliver Cromwell. At five past she has still not come. Fear seizes him again: she has changed her mind. He walks to the north side and gets a pale pleasure from identifying St Martin's, whose bells once chimed "You owe me three farthings." Then he sees her at the monument's base, reading or pretending to read a spiral poster. It is not safe to go near until more people gather. Telescreens ring the pediment. Then shouting and a zoom of heavy vehicles rise to the left. Everyone seems to run across the square. She nips round the lions and joins the rush; he follows.
