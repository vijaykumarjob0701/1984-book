## Summary

...hated it, but nothing would make her stop. She used to call it — Julia will never guess. "Our duty to the Party," Julia says at once. How did she know? She has been at school too: sex talks once a month for the over-sixteens, and in the Youth Movement. They rub it in for years. It works in a lot of cases, but you can never tell; people are such hypocrites. She enlarges on the subject. With Julia everything comes back to her own sexuality; as soon as that is touched she is capable of great acuteness. Unlike Winston she has grasped the inner meaning of the Party's sexual puritanism. It is not merely that the sex instinct creates a world of its own outside Party control and must therefore be destroyed if possible. More important, sexual privation induces hysteria, which is desirable because it can be turned into war-fever and leader-worship. The way she puts it: when you make love you use up energy; afterwards you feel happy and don't give a damn. They cannot bear that. They want you bursting with energy all the time. All the marching, cheering, and flag-waving is simply sex gone sour. If you are happy inside yourself, why get excited about Big Brother, Three-Year Plans, the Two Minutes Hate, and the rest of their rot? That is very true, he thinks. There is a direct intimate connexion between chastity and political orthodoxy. How could the fear, hatred, and lunatic credulity the Party needs be kept at the right pitch except by bottling down some powerful instinct and using it as a driving force? The sex impulse was dangerous, so the Party turned it to account. They played a similar trick with parenthood. The family could not actually be abolished, and people were even encouraged to be fond of their children in almost the old way.
