## Summary

...live in, and the strategy by which they shall destroy it. When they have read the book, they will be full members of the Brotherhood. But between the general aims they are fighting for and the immediate tasks of the moment, they will never know anything. He tells them the Brotherhood exists, but he cannot tell whether it numbers a hundred members or ten million. From their personal knowledge they will never be able to say that it numbers even as many as a dozen. They will have three or four contacts, renewed from time to time as they disappear. As this was their first contact, it will be preserved. When they receive orders, the orders will come from him. If it is necessary to communicate, it will be through Martin. When they are finally caught, they will confess. That is unavoidable. But they will have very little to confess other than their own actions. They will not be able to betray more than a handful of unimportant people. Probably they will not even betray him. By that time he may be dead, or he will have become a different person, with a different face. He continues to move to and fro over the soft carpet. In spite of the bulkiness of his body there is a remarkable grace in his movements. It comes out even in the gesture with which he thrusts a hand into his pocket, or manipulates a cigarette. More even than of strength, he gives an impression of confidence and of an understanding tinged by irony. However much in earnest he might be, he has nothing of the single-mindedness that belongs to a fanatic. When he speaks of murder, suicide, venereal disease, amputated limbs, and altered faces, it is with a faint air of persiflage. The tone says the horrors are unavoidable and must be done unflinchingly — but they are not what the work will be when life is worth living again. A wave of admiration, almost of worship, flows out from Winston toward O'Brien. For the moment he has forgotten the shadowy figure of Goldstein. When you look at O'Brien's powerful shoulders and his blunt-featured face, so ugly and yet so civilized, it is impossible to believe that he could be defeated.
