## Summary

...ment in her appearance is startling. A few dabs of colour in the right places have made her not only much prettier but, above all, far more feminine. Short hair and boyish overalls only add to the effect. As he takes her in his arms a wave of synthetic violets floods his nostrils. He remembers the half-dark basement kitchen and a woman's cavernous mouth. It is the very same scent; at the moment it does not seem to matter. Scent too! Yes, dear, scent too. And next she is going to get a real woman's frock and wear it instead of these bloody trousers — silk stockings and high-heeled shoes. In this room she is going to be a woman, not a Party comrade. They fling their clothes off and climb into the huge mahogany bed. It is the first time he has stripped naked in her presence. Until now he has been too ashamed of his pale, meagre body, varicose veins on the calves, the discoloured patch over his ankle. There are no sheets, but the blanket is threadbare and smooth, and the size and springiness of the bed astonish both of them. Sure to be full of bugs, she says, but who cares? One never sees a double bed nowadays except in prole homes. Winston slept in one in boyhood; Julia never has, so far as she remembers. Presently they sleep a little. When he wakes the clock has crept to nearly nine. He does not stir, because her head is in the crook of his arm. Most of her make-up has transferred to his face or the bolster, but a light stain of rouge still brings out the beauty of her cheekbone. A yellow ray from the sinking sun falls across the foot of the bed and lights the fireplace, where the water is boiling fast. Down in the yard the woman has stopped singing, but faint shouts of children float in. He wonders vaguely whether in the abolished past it was a normal experience to lie like this, in the cool of a summer evening, a man and a woman with no clothes on, making love when they chose, talking of what they chose, not...
