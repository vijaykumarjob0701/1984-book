## Summary

The song finishes: there they lie, and here we lie, under the spreading chestnut tree. The three men do not move, but Rutherford's ruined face is full of tears. For the first time Winston notices, with an inward shudder he cannot explain, that Aaronson and Rutherford have broken noses. A little later all three are re-arrested, said to have started new plots the moment they were freed. At a second trial they confess the old crimes plus a string of new ones. They are executed, and the Party histories record their fate as a warning. About five years later, in 1973, a forgotten scrap slips out of Winston's pneumatic tube with other documents. Flattened, it is half a page of *The Times* from about ten years earlier, date included, showing delegates at a Party function in New York. Jones, Aaronson, and Rutherford sit prominently in the middle; their names are in the caption. The point is that at both trials all three had confessed that on that date they were on Eurasian soil — a secret flight from Canada to Siberia to meet the Eurasian General Staff and betray military secrets. Winston remembers the date because it was midsummer day. There is only one conclusion: the confessions were lies. That is not news to him even then — he never thought purge victims had done what they confessed — but this is concrete evidence, a fragment of the abolished past, like a fossil bone in the wrong...
