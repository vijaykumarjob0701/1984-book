## Summary

...solid men in black uniforms, with iron-shod boots on their feet and truncheons in their hands. Winston is not trembling any longer. Even his eyes he barely moves. One thing alone matters: to keep still, to keep still and not give them an excuse to hit you. A man with a smooth prize-fighter's jowl in which the mouth is only a slit pauses opposite him, balancing his truncheon meditatively between thumb and forefinger. Winston meets his eyes. The feeling of nakedness, with one's hands behind one's head and one's face and body all exposed, is almost unbearable. The man protrudes the tip of a white tongue, licks the place where his lips should have been, and then passes on. There is another crash. Someone has picked up the glass paperweight from the table and smashed it to pieces on the hearth-stone. The fragment of coral, a tiny crinkle of pink like a sugar rosebud from a cake, rolls across the mat. How small, thought Winston, how small it always was! There is a gasp and a thump behind him, and he receives a violent kick on the ankle which nearly flings him off his balance. One of the men has smashed his fist into Julia's solar plexus, doubling her up like a pocket ruler. She is thrashing about on the floor, fighting for breath. Winston dares not turn his head even by a millimetre, but sometimes her livid, gasping face comes within the angle of his vision. Even in his terror it is as though he can feel the pain in his own body, the deadly pain which nevertheless is less urgent than the struggle to get back her breath. He knows what it is like: the terrible, agonizing pain which is there all the while but cannot be suffered yet, because before all else it is necessary to be able to breathe. Then two of the men hoist her up by knees and shoulders and carry her out of the room like a sack. Winston has a glimpse of her face, upside down, yellow and contorted, with the eyes shut, and still with a smear of rouge on either cheek; and that is the last he sees of her. He stands dead still. No one has hit him yet. Thoughts which come of their own accord but seem totally uninteresting begin to flit through his mind. He wonders whether they have got Mr Charrington.
