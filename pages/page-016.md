## Summary

The sandy-haired woman turns pink, her mouth opening and shutting like a fish. Even O'Brien's face flushes as he sits rigid, chest heaving. The dark-haired girl screams "Swine!" and hurls a Newspeak dictionary at Goldstein's nose; the voice goes on. Winston finds himself shouting and kicking his chair. The horror of the Hate is not that you must pretend — you cannot help joining. In seconds, fear and the wish to smash and torture sweep the room like current and turn people into screaming lunatics against their will. The anger is abstract and can be pointed at any target, like a flame. For a moment Winston hates Big Brother, the Party, and the Thought Police, and pities Goldstein as the last sane heretic. The next moment he adores Big Brother as a rock-like protector and sees Goldstein as a sinister wizard who could wreck civilisation with his voice. Hatred can even be moved by a deliberate effort. With a nightmare-like wrench, Winston starts to shift his hate from the face on the screen.
