## Summary

The Party's last, most essential command is to reject the evidence of your eyes and ears. Winston's heart sinks at the enormous power lined up against him: any Party intellectual could overthrow him in debate with subtle arguments he could neither understand nor answer. And yet he is sure he is right and they are wrong. The obvious, the silly, and the true must be defended. Truisms are true — hold on to that. The solid world exists; its laws do not change. Stones are hard, water is wet, unsupported objects fall toward the earth's centre. Feeling that he is speaking to O'Brien and stating an important axiom, he writes that freedom is the freedom to say that two plus two make four. If that is granted, all else follows. Chapter 7 ends on that sentence.
