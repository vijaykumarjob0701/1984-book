## Summary

There's been a lot of tea about lately — they've captured India, or something, she says vaguely. But listen: she wants him to turn his back for three minutes, sit on the other side of the bed, not too near the window, and not turn till she tells him. Winston gazes abstractedly through the muslin. Down in the yard the red-armed woman is still marching between tub and line. She takes two more pegs from her mouth and sings that they say time heals all things, they say you can always forget, but the smiles and tears across the years still twist her heart-strings. She knows the whole drivelling song by heart. Her voice floats up with the sweet summer air, very tuneful, charged with a sort of happy melancholy. One feels she would have been perfectly content, if the June evening were endless and the wash inexhaustible, to stay a thousand years pegging diapers and singing rubbish. He has never heard a Party member singing alone and spontaneously. It would even have seemed slightly unorthodox, a dangerous eccentricity, like talking to oneself. Perhaps only people near starvation have anything to sing about. "You can turn round now." He turns, and for a second almost fails to recognise her. He had actually expected to see her naked. She is not. The transformation is much more surprising. She has painted her face. She must have slipped into a prole shop and bought a complete set of make-up. Lips deeply reddened, cheeks rouged, nose powdered; even a touch under the eyes to make them brighter. It is not very skilfully done, but his standards are not high.
