## Summary

Chapter 3 begins with Winston dreaming of his mother. He thinks he was ten or eleven when she vanished. He remembers her as tall, quiet, and statuesque, with splendid fair hair; his father is vaguer — dark, thin, neat, thin-soled shoes, glasses. Both were probably taken in an early 1950s purge. In the dream his mother sits far below with his baby sister, a tiny silent child with large watchful eyes. They look up from a place like a well, a grave, or the saloon of a sinking ship, still able to see him as green water closes over them. He is in light and air; they are sucked down because he is above. There is no blame, only the knowledge that they must die so he can live, as if that were the natural order. He does not remember the facts, but the dream tells him their lives were sacrificed for his. It is the kind of dream that continues waking thought. What strikes him is that his mother's death, nearly thirty years ago, was tragic in a way that is no longer possible.
