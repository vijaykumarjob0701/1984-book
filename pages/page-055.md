## Summary

She had never seen shoes like that, so he was probably a foreigner — pretty smart for a seven-year-old, Parsons says. Winston asks what happened to the man. Parsons will not say, but mimes aiming a rifle and clicks his tongue. Syme, not looking up, says "Good." Winston dutifully agrees they cannot take chances. Parsons adds that there is a war on. A trumpet from the telescreen seems to confirm it, but the announcement is from the Ministry of Plenty, not a battle victory. A young voice cries that they have won the battle for production: living standards are up 20 percent, and "irrepressible spontaneous demonstrations" thanked Big Brother for a new happy life. Figures on foodstuffs begin. The phrase "our new, happy life" keeps returning; it has lately been a Ministry favourite. Parsons listens with gaping solemnity, a kind of edified boredom: he cannot follow the numbers but knows they are a cause for satisfaction. He pulls out a huge filthy pipe already half full of charred tobacco; at 100 grams a week you can rarely fill a pipe. Winston holds a Victory Cigarette carefully horizontal. The new ration starts tomorrow and he has only four left. For the moment he shuts his ears to remoter noise and listens to the telescreen.
