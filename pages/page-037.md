## Summary

Chapter 4 opens at Winston's work. He sighs without meaning to, pulls the speakwrite close, blows dust from the mouthpiece, and puts on his glasses. Four small paper cylinders have already dropped from the pneumatic tube on his right; he unrolls and clips them. The cubicle has three openings: a small tube for written messages, a larger one for newspapers, and a grated oblong slit in the side wall for waste. Those slits, all over the building, are nicknamed memory holes. Any document due for destruction, or even loose scrap, is dropped in and whisked on warm air to hidden furnaces. The four slips are one or two lines of Ministry jargon, mostly Newspeak. They order him to fix a misreported Big Brother speech about Africa, check forecast "misprints" against the current issue, correct a Ministry of Plenty chocolate "misquote," and fully rewrite a December report of Big Brother's day-order that is "doubleplusungood" and refers to unpersons, then send it up before filing.
