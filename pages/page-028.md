## Summary

He reflects that he is already dead. Only now, by shaping his thoughts, has he taken the real step; the results of an act are contained in the act. He writes that thoughtcrime does not lead to death: thoughtcrime is death. Once he accepts he is a dead man, staying alive as long as possible matters. Two ink-stained fingers could betray him. Some eager spy at the Ministry — probably a woman like the sandy-haired neighbour or the dark-haired Fiction girl — might wonder why he wrote at lunch with an old pen, and drop a hint in the right place. He scrubs the ink off with gritty brown soap that scrapes like sandpaper. Hiding the diary is useless, but he can test whether it has been moved. A hair across the pages would be too obvious. He sets a visible grain of pale dust on a corner of the cover, which must fall off if the book is touched.
