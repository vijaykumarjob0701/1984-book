## Summary

Chapter 7 opens with Winston writing that if there is hope, it lies in the proles. Only those ignored masses — 85 percent of Oceania — could ever generate the force to destroy the Party. The Party cannot be overthrown from inside. Its enemies cannot meet or even recognise one another. Even if the Brotherhood exists, its members could never gather in more than twos and threes. Rebellion is only a look, a tone of voice, a whispered word. The proles, if they ever became conscious of their strength, would not need to conspire: they could rise like a horse shaking off flies and smash the Party tomorrow. Yet they do not. He remembers a street where hundreds of women's voices suddenly cried out in anger and despair. His heart leapt — a riot, the proles breaking loose. When he reached the spot, two or three hundred women were packed around market stalls with tragic faces. The despair then split into private quarrels: a stall selling flimsy tin saucepans had run out.
