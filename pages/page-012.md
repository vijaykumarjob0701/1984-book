## Summary

Cramp stops him. He cannot see why he wrote that "rubbish," but another memory has become clear: this morning's incident is why he came home to start the diary. Around eleven hundred, the Records Department is moving chairs for the Two Minutes Hate. Two people he knows only by sight walk in. One is a dark-haired woman of about twenty-seven from the Fiction Department, often seen with oily hands and a spanner, so she probably services the novel-writing machines. She looks bold and athletic and wears the Junior Anti-Sex League's tight scarlet sash. Winston has disliked her from the start. She seems to carry a world of hockey, cold baths, hikes, and "clean-mindedness." He generally distrusts young pretty women as the keenest Party believers and amateur spies. This one feels especially dangerous; a corridor glance once terrified him, and he has wondered if she works for the Thought Police.
