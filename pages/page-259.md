## Summary

In Winston's case, O'Brien says, the worst thing happens to be rats. A nameless tremor had already passed through him; now the meaning of the mask hits. His body fails. He cries that they cannot do that. O'Brien recalls the dream: a wall of blackness, a roar, something terrible on the other side that Winston would not name. It was the rats. Winston tries to bargain — this is not necessary; what do they want him to do? O'Brien talks past him like a lecturer. Pain is not always enough; some people can stand pain to the point of death. For everyone there is something unendurable, not a matter of courage. Clutching a rope as you fall is instinct. For Winston, rats are that pressure. He will do what is required. Winston still does not know what "it" is. O'Brien carries the cage to the nearer table.
