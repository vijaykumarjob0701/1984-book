## Summary

...Winston. You believe that reality is something objective, external, existing in its own right. You also believe that the nature of reality is self-evident. When you delude yourself into thinking that you see something, you assume that everyone else sees the same thing as you. But I tell you, Winston, that reality is not external. Reality exists in the human mind, and nowhere else. Not in the individual mind, which can make mistakes, and in any case soon perishes: only in the mind of the Party, which is collective and immortal. Whatever the Party holds to be the truth, is truth. It is impossible to see reality except by looking through the eyes of the Party. That is the fact that you have got to relearn, Winston. It needs an act of self-destruction, an effort of the will. You must humble yourself before you can become sane. He pauses for a few moments, as though to allow what he has been saying to sink in. Does Winston remember writing in his diary that freedom is the freedom to say that two plus two make four? Yes, says Winston. O'Brien holds up his left hand, its back towards Winston, with the thumb hidden and the four fingers extended. How many fingers is he holding up? Four. And if the Party says that it is not four but five — then how many? Four. The word ends in a gasp of pain. The needle of the dial has shot up to fifty-five. The sweat has sprung out all over Winston's body. The air tears into his lungs and issues again in deep groans which even by clenching his teeth he cannot stop. O'Brien watches him, the four fingers still extended. He draws back the lever. This time the pain is only slightly eased. How many fingers, Winston? Four. The needle goes up to sixty. How many fingers, Winston? Four! Four! What else can I say? Four! The needle must have risen again, but he does not look at it. The heavy, stern face and the four fingers fill his vision. | Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| "reality is not external" | O'Brien's claim: truth is not "out there" |
| "only in the mind of the Party" | a single person's mind can err and die; the Party mind cannot |
| "looking through the eyes of the Party" | you can only "see" what the Party sees |
| "How many fingers am I holding up" | the test: deny what is in front of his eyes |
