## Summary

...beer hits him in the face. As he enters, the noise drops by about half. He feels everyone eyeing his blue overalls. A darts game at the far end pauses for as much as thirty seconds. The old man is at the bar, arguing with a large, stout, hook-nosed young barman with huge forearms. A knot of drinkers watches. The old man says he asked civilly for a pint mug. The barman asks what in hell a pint is. The old man is disgusted: a pint is half a quart, four quarts to the gallon — next the barman will need the alphabet. The barman has never heard of those measures; they serve litre and half-litre only. The old man persists: he likes a pint; they did not have "bleeding litres" when he was young. The barman jokes that when the old man was young they all lived in the treetops. The room laughs, and the unease caused by Winston's entry fades. The old man's white-stubbled face flushes; he turns away muttering and bumps into Winston. Winston takes his arm and offers a drink. "You're a gent," the old man says, not noticing the overalls, and aggressively orders a pint of wallop. The barman swishes two half-litres of dark beer into thick glasses rinsed in a bucket.
