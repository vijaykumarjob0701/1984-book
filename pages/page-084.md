## Summary

...the great mass of people never had enough to eat from birth to death. Half had no boots. They worked twelve hours a day, left school at nine, slept ten in a room. At the same time a few thousand rich "capitalists" owned everything, lived in gorgeous houses with thirty servants, rode in cars and four-horse carriages, drank champagne, wore top hats. The old man brightens at "top hats." Funny Winston should mention them: only yesterday he thought he had not seen one in years — gone right out. The last time he wore one was at his sister-in-law's funeral, hired, maybe fifty years ago. Winston says the hats are not the point. Those capitalists, with a few lawyers and priests living on them, were lords of the earth. Ordinary workers were their slaves. They could ship you to Canada like cattle, sleep with your daughters, have you flogged with a cat-o'-nine-tails. You had to take your cap off. Every capitalist went about with a gang of lackeys. The old man brightens again at "lackeys" — a word he has not heard in donkey's years. He used to go to Hyde Park on Sunday afternoons to hear speakers: Salvation Army, Roman Catholics, Jews, Indians, all sorts. One powerful speaker really gave it to them: "lackeys of the bourgeoisie," "flunkies of the ruling class," parasites, and...

## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| gorgeous | splendid and richly decorated |
| hired | rented for one occasion |
| lackeys | servants who wait on the powerful |
| recollect | remember |
| blokes | informal word for men |
| bourgeoisie | the owning / middle class (political speech) |
| flunkies | another insulting word for servants of the rich |
| parasites | people who live off others' work |
