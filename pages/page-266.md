## Summary

He wonders if the dislike is only the past, or also his bloated, watering face. They sit on iron chairs, not too close. She crushes a twig with a clumsy shoe. "I betrayed you," she says. "I betrayed you," he answers. Sometimes, she explains, they threaten you with something you cannot stand. You say do it to somebody else — and you might pretend later it was a trick. It is not true. At the time you mean it; you want it to happen to the other person. All you care about is yourself. He echoes her. After that you do not feel the same. There is nothing more to say. She mentions her Tube and stands. "We must meet again," they both say, without heat. He trails behind, then the cold walk feels pointless. What he wants is not to leave her so much as to get back to the Chestnut Tree, which has never seemed so attractive.
