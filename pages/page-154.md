## Summary

Chapter 8 opens: they have done it, they have done it at last. The room they are standing in is long-shaped and softly lit. The telescreen is dimmed to a low murmur; the dark-blue carpet is so rich it feels like treading on velvet. At the far end O'Brien sits at a table under a green-shaded lamp, papers piled on either side. He has not bothered to look up when the servant showed Julia and Winston in. Winston's heart is thumping so hard he doubts he will be able to speak. They have done it at last is all he can think. It was a rash act to come here at all, and sheer folly to arrive together — though they came by different routes and only met on O'Brien's doorstep. Merely to walk into such a place needs an effort of the nerve. Only on very rare occasions does one see inside Inner Party dwellings, or even penetrate the quarter where they live. The whole atmosphere of the huge block — richness, spaciousness, unfamiliar smells of good food and good tobacco, silent incredibly rapid lifts, white-jacketed servants hurrying to and fro — is intimidating. Although he has a good pretext for coming, he is haunted at every step by the fear that a black-uniformed guard will appear, demand his papers, and order him out. O'Brien's servant, however, has admitted them without demur: a small, dark-haired man in a white jacket, with a diamond-shaped, completely expressionless face. The passage is softly carpeted, with cream-papered walls and white wainscoting, all exquisitely clean. That too is intimidating. Winston cannot remember ever seeing a passageway whose walls were not grimy from the contact of human bodies. O'Brien has a slip of paper between his fingers and seems to be studying it intently.
