## Summary

Winston feels the act of marking the paper will be the point of no return. In clumsy small letters he writes the date: April 4th, 1984. Helplessness follows. He is not even sure the year is 1984 — he thinks he is thirty-nine and was born around 1944 or 1945, but dates can no longer be fixed within a year or two. He wonders who the diary is for, and decides it is for a future that does not yet exist. That thought collides with the Newspeak idea of doublethink. Talking to the future seems impossible: if the future is like now, it will not listen; if it is different, his situation will make no sense. He stares at the page. The telescreen switches to loud military music. For weeks he believed only courage was needed to copy down the endless private monologue in his head, but now even that inner talk has stopped. His ulcer itches; he must not scratch it. He notices only the blank page, the itch, the music, and a mild gin dizziness.
