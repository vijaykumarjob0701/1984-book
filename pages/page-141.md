But she only questions Party teachings when they touch her own life. Often she accepts the official mythology because the difference between truth and falsehood does not seem important. She believes, from school, that the Party invented aeroplanes. Winston remembers that in his late-fifties schooldays the Party only claimed the helicopter; a dozen years later, in Julia's schooldays, it already claimed the aeroplane; one generation more and it will claim the steam engine. When he tells her aeroplanes existed before he was born and long before the Revolution, she finds the fact totally uninteresting. Who cares who invented them? More shocking is a chance remark: she does not remember that four years ago Oceania was at war with Eastasia and at peace with Eurasia. She regards the whole war as a sham, but she has not even noticed the enemy's name changing. "I thought we'd always been at war with Eurasia," she says vaguely. That frightens him a little. Aeroplanes predate her birth, but the switch happened only four years ago, after she was grown. He argues for perhaps a quarter of an hour and finally forces her memory back until she dimly recalls Eastasia as the former enemy. She still finds it unimportant. "Who cares?" she says. It is always one bloody war after another, and one knows the news is all lies anyway. Sometimes he talks about the Records Department and the impudent forgeries he commits there.
