## Summary

Part Three opens: he does not know where he is. Presumably he is in the Ministry of Love, but there is no way to be certain. He is in a high-ceilinged windowless cell with walls of glittering white porcelain. Concealed lamps flood it with cold light, and a low steady humming sound seems connected with the air supply. A bench just wide enough to sit on runs round the wall, broken only by the door and, opposite, a lavatory pan with no wooden seat. There are four telescreens, one in each wall. There is a dull ache in his belly, there ever since they bundled him into the closed van. He is also hungry with a gnawing, unwholesome hunger. It might be twenty-four hours since he ate, or thirty-six. He still does not know, and probably never will, whether it was morning or evening when they arrested him. Since the arrest he has not been fed. He sits as still as he can, hands crossed on his knee. He has already learned to sit still. Unexpected movements bring yells from the telescreen. The craving for food grows. What he longs for above all is a piece of bread. He has an idea there are a few breadcrumbs in his overall pocket — even, because something sometimes tickles his leg, a sizeable bit of crust. In the end temptation overcomes fear; he slips a hand into his pocket. "Smith!" yells a voice from the telescreen. "6079 Smith W.! Hands out of pockets in the cells!" He sits still again. Before being brought here he was taken to another place that must have been an ordinary prison or a temporary lock-up used by the patrols.
