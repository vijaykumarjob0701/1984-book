## Summary

...stood up before his eyes like pillars, enormous, blurry, and seeming to vibrate, but unmistakably four. How many fingers, Winston? Four! Stop it, stop it! How can you go on? Four! Four! How many fingers, Winston? Five! Five! Five! No, Winston, that is no use. You are lying. You still think there are four. How many fingers, please? Four! Five! Four! Anything you like. Only stop it, stop the pain! Abruptly he is sitting up with O'Brien's arm round his shoulders. He has perhaps lost consciousness for a few seconds. The bonds that held his body down are loosened. He feels very cold, he is shaking uncontrollably, his teeth are chattering, the tears are rolling down his cheeks. For a moment he clings to O'Brien like a baby, curiously comforted by the heavy arm round his shoulders. He has the feeling that O'Brien is his protector, that the pain is something that came from outside, from some other source, and that it was O'Brien who would save him from it. "You are a slow learner, Winston," says O'Brien gently. How can he help it? he blubbers. How can he help seeing what is in front of his eyes? Two and two are four. Sometimes, Winston. Sometimes they are five. Sometimes they are three. Sometimes they are all of them at once. You must try harder. It is not easy to become sane. He lays Winston down on the bed. The grip of his limbs tightens again, but the pain has ebbed away and the trembling has stopped, leaving him merely weak and cold. O'Brien motions with his head to the man in the white coat, who has stood immobile throughout the proceedings. The man in the white coat bends down and looks closely into Winston's eyes, feels his pulse, lays an ear against his chest, taps here and there, then nods to O'Brien. "Again," says O'Brien.
