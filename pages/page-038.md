## Summary

Winston sets the fourth, more complex job aside with faint satisfaction. The other three are routine, though the forecasts will mean boring figure-work. He dials "back numbers" and *The Times* issues slide from the tube. Officially he must "rectify" items. On 17 March Big Brother predicted a quiet South Indian front and a Eurasian attack in North Africa; the attack came in South India instead, so the speech must be rewritten to "predict" what happened. December forecasts of consumer goods for the last quarter of 1983 (also the sixth quarter of the Ninth Three-Year Plan) were wildly wrong compared with today's claimed output; Winston must make the old numbers match the new ones. The third slip is quick: in February the Ministry of Plenty gave a "categorical pledge" of no chocolate cut in 1984, but the ration will fall from thirty grams to twenty this week. He only needs to replace the promise with a warning that an April cut might be necessary. After each job he clips the speakwritten correction to the paper and sends it down the tube.
