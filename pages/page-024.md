## Summary

Mrs Parsons says the children are noisy because they could not go to a hanging: she is too busy, and Tom will be late. The boy roars to go; the girl chants and dances. Winston remembers that Eurasian prisoners are to be hanged in the Park that evening — a monthly public show children beg to attend. He leaves, but a few steps down the passage something hits his neck like a red-hot wire. Mrs Parsons hauls her son back as the boy pockets a catapult and bellows "Goldstein!" Winston is most struck by the helpless fright on her grey face. At home he hurries past the telescreen, rubbing his neck. The music has stopped; a clipped military voice gloats over a new Floating Fortress anchored between Iceland and the Faroes. He thinks the wretched woman lives in terror of her children, who will soon watch her for unorthodoxy. Nearly all children now seem horrible. Groups like the Spies turn them into wild little savages who still adore Party discipline. Hiking, dummy rifles, slogans, and Big Brother are a glorious game; their cruelty points outward.
