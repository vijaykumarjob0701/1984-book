## Summary

It is 07:15, getting-up time for office workers. Winston drags himself naked from bed — Outer Party members get only 3,000 clothing coupons a year, and pyjamas cost 600 — and grabs a dingy singlet and shorts. Physical Jerks start in three minutes. A violent waking cough empties his lungs; he must lie on his back and gasp. His veins swell and the ulcer itches. A sharp female voice calls the thirty-to-forty group to their places. He springs to attention before a scrawny, muscular instructress in tunic and gym-shoes. She barks arm-bending exercises and tells them to put life into it. The dream is still with him; the rhythm even brings it back. While he pumps his arms with the required look of grim enjoyment, he tries to think back into early childhood. It is extremely hard. Beyond the late 1950s everything fades. Without outside records, even your own life loses shape: you remember events that may never have happened, details without atmosphere, and long blanks. Even country names and map shapes were different. Airstrip One was once called England or Britain. He is fairly sure London was always London.
