## Summary

...almost unthinkable. Party women all seem the same. Chastity is as deeply fixed in them as Party loyalty. Early conditioning — games, cold water, school rubbish, the Spies, the Youth League, lectures, parades, songs, slogans, martial music — has driven natural feeling out. His reason says there must be exceptions; his heart does not believe it. They are impregnable, as the Party meant. What he wants, even more than to be loved, is to break that wall of virtue once in his life. A sexual act successfully performed is rebellion. Desire is thoughtcrime. Even waking Katharine, if he could have, would have been like a seduction, though she was his wife. The rest of the story must be written. He writes that he turned up the lamp, and when he saw her in the light — After darkness the weak paraffin lamp seemed bright. For the first time he sees her properly. He steps forward, then stops, full of lust and terror. He is painfully aware of the risk; patrols might catch him leaving, or already be outside. If he goes without even doing what he came for — ! It has to be written, has to be confessed. In the lamplight he sees that the woman is old. Paint is plastered so thick it might crack like cardboard. There is white in her hair. The worst detail: her mouth hangs a little open on a cavernous blackness. She has no teeth. He writes hurriedly that she was fifty at least, but he went ahead anyway.
