## Summary

Chapter 9 opens with Winston gelatinous with fatigue. Gelatinous is the right word; it came into his head of its own accord. His body seems to have not only the weakness of a jelly but its translucency. He feels that if he held up his hand he would see the light through it. All the blood and lymph have been drained out of him by an enormous debauch of work, leaving only a frail structure of nerves, bones, and skin. All sensations seem magnified. His overalls fret his shoulders, the pavement tickles his feet, even opening and closing a hand makes his joints creak. He has worked more than ninety hours in five days. So has everyone else in the Ministry. Now it is all over, and he has literally nothing to do — no Party work of any description — until tomorrow morning. He can spend six hours in the hiding-place and another nine in his own bed. Slowly, in mild afternoon sunshine, he walks up a dingy street toward Mr Charrington's shop, one eye open for patrols, but irrationally convinced that this afternoon no one will interfere with him. The heavy brief-case bumps his knee at each step, sending a tingling up and down the skin of his leg. Inside it is the book, which he has now had for six days and has not yet opened, nor even looked at. On the sixth day of Hate Week, after processions, speeches, shouting, singing, banners, posters, films, waxworks, drums, trumpets, marching feet, tank tracks, massed planes, booming guns — after six days of this, when the great orgasm was quivering to its climax and hatred of Eurasia had boiled into such delirium that if the crowd could have got their hands on the two thousand Eurasian war-criminals due to be publicly hanged they would have torn them to pieces — at just that moment it was announced that Oceania was not after all at war with Eurasia. Oceania was at war with Eastasia. Eurasia was an ally.
