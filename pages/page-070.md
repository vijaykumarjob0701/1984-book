## Summary

...through dull jobs, fighting for a place on the Tube, darning a sock, cadging a saccharine tablet, saving a cigarette end. The Party's ideal is huge and glittering: steel, concrete, monstrous machines, weapons, a nation of identical warriors and fanatics, three hundred million people with one face. The reality is decaying cities, underfed people in leaky shoes, patched nineteenth-century houses that smell of cabbage and bad lavatories. He sees London as a vast ruin of dustbins, mixed with Mrs Parsons helpless at a blocked pipe. Day and night the telescreens bruise your ears with statistics: more food, clothes, houses, recreations; people living longer, working less, bigger, healthier, happier, better educated than fifty years ago. None of it can be proved or disproved. The Party says 40 percent of adult proles can now read, against 15 percent before; infant deaths are 160 per thousand, against 300. It is like one equation with two unknowns. Every history-book word, even the ones you accept, might be fantasy. For all he knows there was never a jus primae noctis, a capitalist, or a top hat. Everything fades. The past is erased, the erasure forgotten, the lie becomes truth.
