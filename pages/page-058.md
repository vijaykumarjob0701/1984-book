## Summary

The next table's quacking starts again after the announcement. Winston suddenly thinks of Mrs Parsons and her dusty, lined face. Within two years those children will denounce her; she will be vaporized. So will Syme, Winston, and O'Brien. Parsons will not. The eyeless quacking man will not. The beetle-men who scuttle through Ministry corridors will not. The dark-haired Fiction girl will not either. He feels he knows instinctively who will survive, though he cannot name the quality that saves them. A violent jerk pulls him out of the daydream: the girl at the next table has turned and is looking at him with sidelong intensity. The instant their eyes meet she looks away. Sweat starts on his backbone; a pang of terror hits and fades, leaving nagging unease. Why is she watching? Why does she follow him? He cannot remember whether she was already at the table. Yesterday she sat right behind him at the Hate with no need to. Perhaps she wanted to check he was shouting loudly enough. He returns to an earlier thought: she is probably not Thought Police, but an amateur spy is the greatest danger. She may have watched him for five minutes, and his face may not have been perfectly controlled. It is terribly dangerous to let thoughts wander in public or...
