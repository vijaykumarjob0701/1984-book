## Summary

...hyenas — he definitely called them hyenas. Of course, the old man adds, the speaker meant the Labour Party. Winston feels they are talking at cross-purposes. He tries again: does the man feel he has more freedom now? Is he treated more like a human being? In the old days, the people at the top — "The House of Lords," the old man puts in — could they treat you as an inferior just because they were rich? Did you have to call them "Sir" and take off your cap? The old man thinks, drinks, then says yes: they liked you to touch your cap; it showed respect. He did not agree, but he did it often enough — had to. Winston quotes the history books: was it usual for those people and their servants to push you off the pavement into the gutter? One of them pushed him once, the old man says, as if it were yesterday. It was Boat Race night, always rowdy. On Shaftesbury Avenue he accidentally bumped a young "gent" in dress shirt, top hat, and black overcoat, zig-zagging drunk. The man told him to look where he was going; he answered back; the man threatened to twist his head off; he said he would call the police — and then got a shove that nearly sent him under a bus. He was young and about to hit back, only — A sense of helplessness takes Winston. The old man's memory is a rubbish-heap of details. You could question him all day without getting any real information.
