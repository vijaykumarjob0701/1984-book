## Summary

Anything political is forced into the B shape: one short, easy word. In the Ministry of Truth, Records becomes RECDEP, Fiction FICDEP, Teleprogrammes TELEDEP. Saving time is not the only point. Early twentieth-century politics already loved telescoped names — the essay lists several historical examples — especially in totalitarian organisations. Newspeak uses that habit on purpose. Shortening a name cuts away the pictures that used to cling to it. A long phrase such as "Communist International" might call up flags, barricades, and a whole history; COMINTERN suggests only a tight body of doctrine, as limited as a chair. MINITRUE likewise raises fewer, more controllable associations than "Ministry of Truth." That is why words are abbreviated whenever possible, and why they must be easy to pronounce.
