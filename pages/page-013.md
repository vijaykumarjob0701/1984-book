## Summary

He admits she is probably not Thought Police, but near her he still feels uneasy fear and dislike. The other arrival is O'Brien, an Inner Party man whose job is so high and distant Winston barely understands it. Black overalls hush the room. O'Brien is large and brutal-looking, yet he has charm; he resettles his glasses in a gesture that feels oddly civilised, like an old nobleman with a snuffbox. Winston has seen him only about a dozen times in as many years and feels strongly drawn to him. He hopes O'Brien is not perfectly orthodox — or at least is intelligent enough to talk to, if the telescreen could be cheated. There is no way to test this. O'Brien checks the time, stays for the Hate, and sits in Winston's row, with a small sandy-haired neighbour between them and the dark-haired girl behind. Then a hideous grinding noise bursts from the telescreen: the Hate has begun.
