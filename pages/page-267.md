## Summary

He sees the corner table, paper, chess, endless gin, and warmth. Not quite by accident he lets a knot of people separate them, makes a weak attempt to catch up, then turns. Fifty metres on, any of a dozen figures might be hers. At the time, she had said, you mean it. He had meant it; he had wished her delivered to the ——. The telescreen music changes, a cracked jeering note, and a voice sings the chestnut song: under the spreading chestnut tree, I sold you and you sold me. Tears come. A waiter refills the glass. Gin has become the element he lives in — night stupor, morning revival from the bottle by the bed. He sleeps until eleven. From fifteen until closing he is a fixture in the café. No whistle, no telescreen scolding. Twice a week, perhaps, he goes to a dusty office.
