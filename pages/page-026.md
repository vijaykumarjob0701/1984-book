## Summary

The announcer claims the victory may bring the war near its end. Winston expects bad news, and after gory figures of a destroyed Eurasian army comes the real point: next week's chocolate ration falls from thirty grams to twenty. The gin wears off and he feels flattened. The telescreen blasts the anthem "Oceania, 'tis for thee"; people should stand, but he is still out of sight. Lighter music follows. At the window he hears a distant rocket bomb — twenty or thirty a week hit London now. The torn INGSOC poster flaps below. He turns over the "sacred principles": Newspeak, doublethink, the mutability of the past. He feels lost on a sea-floor, himself the monster, alone, the past dead and the future unimaginable. He cannot know if any living person is on his side, or that the Party will not last forever. The three slogans answer him from the Ministry of Truth. He takes out a twenty-five cent coin: the same slogans are on one face, Big Brother's head on the other, eyes still chasing him. Those eyes are on coins, stamps, book covers.
