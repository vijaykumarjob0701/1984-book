## Summary

"Oh, rubbish!" Julia says. Which would he sooner sleep with, her or a skeleton? Doesn't he enjoy being alive? Doesn't he like feeling: this is me, this is my hand, this is my leg, I'm real, I'm solid, I'm alive? Doesn't he like this? She twists round and presses against him. He can feel her through the overalls; her body seems to pour some of its youth and vigour into his. Yes, he likes that. Then stop talking about dying. And now listen: they have to fix the next meeting. They may as well go back to the place in the wood; they have given it a good long rest. But he must get there by a different way this time. She has it all planned. He takes the train — look, she will draw it out. In her practical way she scrapes together a small square of dust and, with a twig from a pigeon's nest, begins drawing a map on the floor. Chapter 3 ends on that practical, living gesture.
