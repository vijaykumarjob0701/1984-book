## Summary

...expressionless voice, as though this were a routine, a sort of catechism, most of whose answers he already knows. Are they prepared to give their lives? Yes. To commit murder? Yes. To commit acts of sabotage which may cause the death of hundreds of innocent people? Yes. To betray their country to foreign powers? Yes. To cheat, forge, blackmail, corrupt the minds of children, distribute habit-forming drugs, encourage prostitution, disseminate venereal diseases — to do anything likely to cause demoralization and weaken the Party? Yes. If, for example, it would somehow serve their interests to throw sulphuric acid in a child's face — are they prepared to do that? Yes. Are they prepared to lose their identity and live out the rest of their lives as a waiter or a dock-worker? Yes. To commit suicide, if and when ordered? Yes. Are they prepared, the two of them, to separate and never see one another again? "No!" breaks in Julia. It appears to Winston that a long time passes before he answers. For a moment he seems even to have been deprived of the power of speech. His tongue works soundlessly, forming the opening syllables first of one word, then of the other, over and over. Until he has said it, he does not know which word he is going to say. "No," he says finally. "You did well to tell me," says O'Brien. It is necessary for them to know everything.

## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| catechism | a set of questions with expected answers |
| sabotage | secret damage to machines, plans, or people |
| forge | make false documents |
| blackmail | force someone by threatening to expose them |
| disseminate | spread widely |
| venereal | sexually transmitted (of disease) |
| demoralization | breaking people's morale and morals |
| sulphuric | a burning industrial acid |
| identity | name, face, and official self |
| syllables | parts of a spoken word |

## Difficult phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| "a sort of catechism" | the questions sound rehearsed, as if he already knows the yeses |
| "weaken the power of the Party" | every horror is justified if it damages the regime |
| "never see one another again" | the one demand they will not accept |
| "which word he was going to say" | even he does not know if he will say yes or no |
| "necessary for us to know everything" | O'Brien treats their refusal as useful information, not a rejection |
