## Summary

...walks on as briskly as if nothing happened. The whole incident lasts less than half a minute. Hiding feeling is already an instinct, and they were standing straight in front of a telescreen. Still it is hard not to show surprise: in the two or three seconds while he helped her up, she slipped something small and flat into his hand on purpose. At the lavatory door he pockets it and feels a scrap of paper folded into a square. At the urinal he unfolds it a little more. There must be a message. For a moment he wants to take it into a water-closet and read it at once. That would be shocking folly: nowhere are telescreens watched more continuously. He goes back to his cubicle, throws the paper casually among the other sheets, puts on his glasses, and pulls the speakwrite over. He tells himself to wait five minutes at least. His heart bangs. Luckily the job is routine figure-work and needs little attention. Whatever is on the paper must have a political meaning. Two possibilities: much the likelier is that she is Thought Police, as he feared. He does not know why they would pass notes this way, but they might. The writing could be a threat, a summons, an order to commit suicide, a trap. The other, wilder idea keeps rising though he tries to crush it: that the message is not from the Thought Police at all, but from some underground organ...
