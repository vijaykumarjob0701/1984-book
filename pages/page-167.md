## Summary

...two or three minutes it is all over. The orator, still gripping the microphone, shoulders hunched, free hand clawing at the air, has gone straight on with his speech. One minute more, and the feral roars of rage are again bursting from the crowd. The Hate continues exactly as before, except that the target has been changed. Looking back, what impresses Winston is that the speaker switched from one line to the other actually in mid-sentence, not only without a pause but without even breaking the syntax. At the moment, though, he has other things to preoccupy him. It is during the disorder while the posters are being torn down that a man whose face he does not see taps him on the shoulder and says he thinks Winston has dropped his brief-case. He takes it abstractedly, without speaking. He knows it will be days before he has a chance to look inside. The instant the demonstration is over he goes straight to the Ministry of Truth, though the time is now nearly twenty-three hours. The entire staff of the Ministry has done likewise. The orders already issuing from the telescreen, recalling them to their posts, are hardly necessary. Oceania is at war with Eastasia: Oceania has always been at war with Eastasia. A large part of the political literature of five years is now completely obsolete. Reports and records of all kinds — newspapers, books, pamphlets, films, sound-tracks, photographs — all have to be rectified at lightning speed. Although no directive is ever issued, it is known that the chiefs of the Department intend that within one week no reference to the war with Eurasia, or the alliance with Eastasia, should remain in existence anywhere. The work is overwhelming, all the more so because the processes it involves cannot be called by their true names. Everyone in Records works eighteen hours in the twenty-four, with two three-hour snatches of sleep. Mattresses are brought up from the cellars and pitched all over the corridors; meals are sandwiches and Victory Coffee wheeled round on trolleys.
