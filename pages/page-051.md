## Summary

Winston almost says "Except the proles," then stops, unsure the remark is orthodox. Syme guesses it anyway and says carelessly that proles are not human beings. By 2050, probably earlier, real knowledge of Oldspeak will be gone. Past literature — Chaucer, Shakespeare, Milton, Byron — will survive only in Newspeak versions that contradict what they once were. Even Party literature and slogans will change. How can you say "freedom is slavery" if the idea of freedom has been abolished? There will be no thought as we know it. Orthodoxy means not thinking — not needing to think. Orthodoxy is unconsciousness. Winston is suddenly sure Syme will be vaporized: he is too intelligent, sees too clearly, speaks too plainly; it is written in his face. Winston finishes his bread and cheese and turns to his coffee. The harsh voice at the next table never stops. A young woman, perhaps the speaker's secretary, keeps agreeing in a silly, eager voice, but he does not pause. Winston knows the man only as someone important in Fiction: about thirty, muscular throat, large mobile mouth. His head is tilted so his glasses are two blank discs.
