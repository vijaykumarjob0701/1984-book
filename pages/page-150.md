## Summary

...up a feeble wail. His mother drew her arm round the child and pressed its face against her breast. Something in the gesture told him that his sister was dying. He turned and fled down the stairs, the chocolate growing sticky in his hand. He never saw his mother again. After he had devoured the chocolate he felt somewhat ashamed and hung about in the streets for several hours, until hunger drove him home. When he came back his mother had disappeared. That was already becoming normal. Nothing was gone from the room except his mother and his sister. They had not taken any clothes, not even his mother's overcoat. To this day he does not know with any certainty that she is dead. It is perfectly possible she was only sent to a forced-labour camp. His sister might have been removed, like Winston himself, to one of the colonies for homeless children — Reclamation Centres, they were called — that grew up after the civil war; or sent to the labour camp with their mother; or simply left somewhere to die. The dream is still vivid, especially the enveloping protecting gesture of the arm in which its whole meaning seemed to be contained. His mind goes back to another dream of two months ago. Exactly as his mother had sat on the dingy white-quilted bed, with the child clinging to her, so she had sat in the sunken ship, far underneath him and drowning deeper every minute, but still looking up through the darkening water. He tells Julia the story of his mother's disappearance. Without opening her eyes she rolls over into a more comfortable position. She expects he was a beastly little swine in those days. All children are swine. Yes, he says. But the real point of the story —— From her breathing it is evident she is going off to sleep again. He would have liked to continue talking about his mother. He does not suppose, from what he remembers, that she was an unusual woman, still less an intelligent one; and yet she had possessed a kind of nobility, a kind of purity, simply because the standards she obeyed were private ones. Her feelings were her own, and could not be altered from outside.
