## Summary

Parsons nudges Winston and jokes that Syme is "keen," working in the lunch hour on something too brainy for him. He has chased Winston for a forgotten "sub" — a subscription. About a quarter of one's salary is earmarked for countless "voluntary" subscriptions. This one is the Hate Week house-by-house fund; Parsons is block treasurer and wants Victory Mansions to have the most flags. Winston hands over two filthy notes, which Parsons records in the neat writing of someone barely literate. He has heard his boy shot at Winston with a catapult and says he gave him a dressing-down, even threatened to take the weapon away. Winston suggests the child was upset about missing the execution. Parsons takes that as the "right spirit" and boasts of both children's keenness for the Spies and the war. Last Saturday his little girl left a hike near Berkhamsted with two others and followed a strange man for two hours through woods, then handed him to patrols in Amersham. Winston is taken aback. Parsons says triumphantly that the child decided he might be an enemy agent dropped by parachute. What put her on to him first was his funny shoes.
