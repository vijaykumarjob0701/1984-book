## Summary

...to teach them Party ideology. Strong political feeling in the proles is not wanted. All that is needed is a primitive patriotism that can be used when longer hours or shorter rations must be accepted. When they are discontented, the feeling goes nowhere: without general ideas they can only pick at small grievances, and the larger evils escape them. Most proles do not even have telescreens. Ordinary police barely bother them. London has a huge criminal underworld of thieves, bandits, prostitutes, drug-sellers, and racketeers, but because it stays among the proles it does not matter. In morals they may follow their old code. Party sexual puritanism is not forced on them; promiscuity is unpunished and divorce is allowed. Even religion would be allowed if they wanted it. They are beneath suspicion. The slogan is that proles and animals are free. Winston cautiously scratches his itching ulcer. He always comes back to the same problem: you cannot know what life before the Revolution was really like. From a children's history book borrowed from Mrs Parsons he copies a passage into the diary. It says that before the "glorious Revolution" London was dark, dirty, and hungry, with thousands unshod and homeless. Children worked twelve hours a day, were whipped, and lived on stale crusts. Among that poverty stood a few great houses...

## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| indoctrinate | train people to accept a set of beliefs |
| ideology | an official system of political ideas |
| primitive | simple and undeveloped |
| patriotism | love of one's country (here, easily used by the Party) |
| discontented | unhappy and complaining |
| grievances | specific complaints |
| criminality | crime as a whole way of life |
| racketeers | people who run illegal money schemes |
| puritanism | a strict, joyless moral code, especially about sex |
| imposed | forced onto people |
| cautiously | carefully, so as not to be noticed |
| stale | old and no longer fresh |
| breadcrusts | the hard outer bits of bread |

## Difficult phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| "a primitive patriotism" | a simple us-versus-them feeling the Party can switch on |
| "petty specific grievances" | small local complaints, not the whole system |
| "They were beneath suspicion" | the Party does not bother watching them as it watches members |
| "Proles and animals are free" | the slogan that "freedom" for proles is the freedom of beasts |
