## Summary

They will have heard rumours of the Brotherhood. No doubt they have formed their own picture of it: a huge underworld of conspirators meeting in cellars, scribbling messages on walls, recognizing one another by codewords or special movements of the hand. Nothing of the kind exists. Members have no way of recognizing one another, and it is impossible for any one member to know the identity of more than a few others. Goldstein himself, if he fell into the hands of the Thought Police, could not give them a complete list of members, or any information that would lead to a complete list. No such list exists. The Brotherhood cannot be wiped out because it is not an organization in the ordinary sense. Nothing holds it together except an idea which is indestructible. They will never have anything to sustain them except the idea. They will get no comradeship and no encouragement. When finally they are caught, they will get no help. The Brotherhood never helps its members. At most, when it is absolutely necessary that someone should be silenced, they are occasionally able to smuggle a razor blade into a prisoner's cell. They will have to get used to living without results and without hope. They will work for a while, they will be caught, they will confess, and then they will die. Those are the only results they will ever see. There is no possibility that any perceptible change will happen within their own lifetime. They are the dead. Their only true life is in the future. They shall take part in it as handfuls of dust and splinters of bone. But how far away that future may be, there is no knowing. It might be a thousand years. At present nothing is possible except to extend the area of sanity little by little. They cannot act collectively. They can only spread knowledge outwards from individual to individual, generation after generation. In the face of the Thought Police there is no other way. He halts and looks for the third time at his wrist-watch. It is almost time for Julia to leave. Wait. The decanter is still half full. He fills the glasses and raises his own glass by the stem.
