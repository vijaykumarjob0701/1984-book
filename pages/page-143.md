## Summary

...lifetime. But one can imagine little knots of resistance springing up, small groups banding together, even leaving a few records so the next generations can carry on. Julia is not interested in the next generation. She is interested in *us*. "You're only a rebel from the waist downwards," he tells her. She thinks this brilliantly witty and flings her arms round him. In the ramifications of Party doctrine she has not the faintest interest. Whenever he talks of Ingsoc, doublethink, the mutability of the past, the denial of objective reality, or Newspeak words, she becomes bored and confused and says she never pays attention to that kind of thing. One knows it is all rubbish, so why worry? She knows when to cheer and when to boo, and that is all one needs. If he persists, she has a disconcerting habit of falling asleep. She can sleep at any hour in any position. Talking to her, he realises how easy it is to present an appearance of orthodoxy while having no grasp of what orthodoxy means. In a way the Party's world-view works best on people incapable of understanding it. They can accept the most flagrant violations of reality because they never grasp the enormity of what is demanded and are not interested enough in public events to notice. By lack of understanding they remain sane. They swallow everything, and it does them no harm, because it leaves no residue — as a grain of corn passes undigested through a bird. Chapter 5 ends on that thought.
