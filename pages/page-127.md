## Summary

...and a sacking apron is stumping between a washtub and a clothes line, pegging out square white babies' diapers. Whenever her mouth is not corked with clothes pegs she sings in a powerful contralto a song about a hopeless fancy that stole her heart away. The tune has been haunting London for weeks: one of countless prole songs published by a Music Department sub-section, words composed with no human intervention on a versificator. But she sings so tunefully that the rubbish becomes almost pleasant. He can hear her, the scrape of shoes on flagstones, children's cries, a faint roar of distant traffic — and yet the room seems curiously silent, thanks to the absence of a telescreen. Folly, folly, folly! It is inconceivable they can frequent this place more than a few weeks without being caught. But the temptation of a hiding-place that is truly their own, indoors and near at hand, has been too much for both of them. For some time after the church belfry they could not arrange meetings. Working hours were drastically increased in anticipation of Hate Week, more than a month away, but the huge preparations throw extra work on everybody. Finally both secure a free afternoon on the same day and agree to go back to the wood. The evening beforehand they meet briefly in the street. As usual Winston hardly looks at her as they drift together, but from the short glance she seems paler than usual.
