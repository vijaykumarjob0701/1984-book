## Summary

Winston stops reading for a moment. Somewhere in remote distance a rocket bomb thunders. The blissful feeling of being alone with the forbidden book, in a room with no telescreen, has not worn off. Solitude and safety are physical sensations, mixed up somehow with the tiredness of his body, the softness of the chair, the faint breeze from the window on his cheek. The book fascinates him, or more exactly it reassures him. In a sense it tells him nothing that is new, but that is part of the attraction. It says what he would have said if he could have set his scattered thoughts in order. It is the product of a mind similar to his own, but enormously more powerful, more systematic, less fear-ridden. The best books, he perceives, are those that tell you what you know already. He has just turned back to Chapter I when he hears Julia's footstep on the stair and starts out of his chair to meet her. She dumps her brown tool-bag on the floor and flings herself into his arms. It is more than a week since they have seen one another. "I've got THE BOOK," he says as they disentangle themselves. "Oh, you've got it? Good," she says without much interest, and almost immediately kneels down beside the oil stove to make the coffee. They do not return to the subject until they have been in bed for half an hour. The evening is just cool enough to make it worthwhile to pull up the counterpane. From below come the familiar sound of singing and the scrape of boots on the flagstones. The brawny red-armed woman Winston saw on his first visit is almost a fixture in the yard. There seems to be no hour of daylight when she is not marching to and fro between the washtub and the line, alternately gagging herself with clothes pegs and breaking forth into lusty song. Julia has settled down on her side and seems already on the point of falling asleep. He reaches out for the book, lying on the floor, and sits up against the bedhead. They must read it, he says. She too. All members of the Brotherhood have to read it. "You read it," she says with her eyes shut. Read it aloud. That is the best way. Then he can explain it to her as he goes.
