## Summary

...hatred, and the bewilderment came crowding back again. But there had been a moment — he does not know how long, thirty seconds, perhaps — of luminous certainty, when each new suggestion of O'Brien's had filled up a patch of emptiness and become absolute truth, and when two and two could have been three as easily as five, if that were what was needed. It had faded out before O'Brien had dropped his hand; but though he could not recapture it, he could remember it, as one remembers a vivid experience at some period of one's life when one was in effect a different person. "You see now," says O'Brien, "that it is at any rate possible." Yes, says Winston. O'Brien stands up with a satisfied air. Over to his left Winston sees the man in the white coat break an ampoule and draw back the plunger of a syringe. O'Brien turns to Winston with a smile. In almost the old manner he resettles his spectacles on his nose. Does Winston remember writing in his diary that it did not matter whether O'Brien was a friend or an enemy, since he was at least a person who understood him and could be talked to? He was right. I enjoy talking to you. Your mind appeals to me. It resembles my own mind except that you happen to be insane. Before we bring the session to an end you can ask me a few questions, if you choose. Any question I like? Anything. He sees that Winston's eyes are upon the dial. It is switched off. What is your first question? "What have you done with Julia?" says Winston. O'Brien smiles again. She betrayed you, Winston. Immediately — unreservedly. I have seldom seen anyone come over to us so promptly. You would hardly recognize her if you saw her. All her rebelliousness, her deceit, her folly, her dirty-mindedness — everything has been burned out of her. It was a perfect conversion, a textbook case. You tortured her? O'Brien leaves this unanswered. Next question. Does Big Brother exist? Of course he exists. The Party exists. Big Brother is the embodiment of the Party.
