## Summary

...to pieces; bread is dark, tea rare, coffee filthy, cigarettes short; only synthetic gin is cheap and plentiful. Age makes it worse, but if the heart sickens at dirt, scarcity, endless winters, sticky socks, dead lifts, cold water, gritty soap, collapsing cigarettes, and evil-tasting food, is that not a sign this is not the natural order? Why feel it intolerable unless some ancestral memory says things were once different? He looks around: nearly everyone is ugly, and would be ugly even out of blue overalls. Alone at a far table a small beetle-like man drinks coffee, eyes darting. It is easy, if you do not look, to believe the Party ideal — tall, sunburnt, carefree youths and deep-bosomed girls — really exists and even predominates. In fact most people in Airstrip One seem small, dark, and ill-favoured. The beetle type multiplies in the Ministries: dumpy men who grow stout early, short-legged, scuttling, with fat unreadable faces and tiny eyes. That type seems to flourish under the Party. The Plenty announcement ends with another trumpet and tinny music. Parsons, vaguely stirred, says Plenty has done a good job and asks for razor blades. Winston repeats the six-week-blade lie. Parsons says he just thought he'd ask.
