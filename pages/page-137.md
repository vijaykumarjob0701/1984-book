## Summary

...children play the Hate Song day and night on a comb and toilet paper, unbearably. Winston's evenings are fuller than ever. Volunteer squads organised by Parsons stitch banners, paint posters, put flagstaffs on roofs, and sling wires across the street for streamers. Parsons boasts that Victory Mansions alone will show four hundred metres of bunting. Heat and manual work give him an excuse to wear shorts and an open shirt; he is in his element, pushing, sawing, hammering, jollying everyone along, and pouring out an endless supply of acrid sweat. A new poster appears all over London with no caption: a huge Eurasian soldier, three or four metres high, expressionless, boots enormous, a sub-machine gun at his hip. From any angle the foreshortened muzzle seems pointed at you. It is pasted on every blank wall and even outnumbers Big Brother's portraits. The usually apathetic proles are being whipped into a burst of patriotism. Rocket bombs are killing more people than usual. One falls on a crowded film theatre in Stepney and buries several hundred; the neighbourhood's long funeral is really an indignation meeting. Another hits a waste-ground playground and blows dozens of children to pieces. There are more angry demonstrations. Goldstein is burned in effigy; hundreds of the soldier posters are torn down and thrown on the flames; shops are looted. | Word | Meaning |

| unbearably | so badly you cannot stand it |
| flagstaffs | poles for flags |
| streamers | long strips of cloth hung for a festival |
| bunting | rows of flags or coloured cloth for display |
| improvising | making do with whatever is at hand |
| exhortations | loud encouraging speeches to the group |
| acrid | sharp and stinging (of sweat or smoke) |
| caption | printed words under or on a picture |
| foreshortening | a drawing trick that makes the gun look aimed at you |
| apathetic | not interested; not caring |
| periodical | happening from time to time |
| indignation | public anger at a supposed outrage |
