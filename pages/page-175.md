## Summary

...dreamed of doing, was not a practicable solution. It conflicted with the almost instinctive worldwide drift toward mechanization, and any country that stayed industrially backward would be helpless in war and dominated by more advanced rivals. Nor was it satisfactory to keep the masses in poverty by restricting the output of goods. That happened to a great extent in the final phase of capitalism, roughly 1920–1940: economies stagnated, land went out of cultivation, equipment was not added to, and great blocks of the population were kept half alive by State charity. But that too meant military weakness, and because the privations were obviously unnecessary it made opposition inevitable. The problem was how to keep the wheels of industry turning without increasing the real wealth of the world. Goods must be produced, but they must not be distributed. In practice the only way of achieving this was continuous warfare. The essential act of war is destruction — not necessarily of human lives, but of the products of human labour. War is a way of shattering to pieces, or pouring into the stratosphere, or sinking in the sea, materials that might otherwise make the masses too comfortable, and hence, in the long run, too intelligent. Even when weapons are not actually destroyed, manufacturing them is still a convenient way of using up labour without producing anything that can be consumed. A Floating Fortress, for example, locks up the labour that would build several hundred cargo-ships. Ultimately it is scrapped as obsolete, never having brought any material benefit to anybody, and with further enormous labours another is built. ## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| practicable | actually possible to carry out |
| mechanization | replacing hand labour with machines |
| stagnate | stop growing or improving |
| privations | forced shortages and hardships |
| stratosphere | the high upper air |
| consumed | used up as ordinary goods |
| obsolete | thrown away as out of date |
| underestimated | officially counted as smaller than they are |

## Difficult phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| "not a practicable solution" | going back to farm life would lose the arms race |
| "Goods must be produced, but they must not be distributed" | factories must run, but comfort must not reach the public |
| "The essential act of war is destruction" | war's real job is to smash the products of work |
| "too comfortable, and hence... too intelligent" | ease would let ordinary people think, which hierarchy cannot allow |
| "eat up any surplus" | the war plan is designed to swallow every leftover after bare survival |
