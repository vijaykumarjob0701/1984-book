## Summary

...Katharine parted. The date that really matters is seven or eight years earlier. The story starts in the mid-1960s, the great purges that wiped out the Revolution's original leaders. By 1970 only Big Brother was left; the rest had been named traitors. Goldstein had fled; others vanished or were executed after show trials and confessions. Among the last survivors were Jones, Aaronson, and Rutherford, arrested around 1965. As often happened they disappeared for a year or more, then were brought out to incriminate themselves. They confessed to dealing with the enemy (then Eurasia), stealing public money, murdering Party members, plotting against Big Brother even before the Revolution, and sabotage that killed hundreds of thousands. After confessing they were pardoned, put back in the Party, and given empty-sounding posts. All three wrote long, grovelling articles in *The Times* explaining their "defection" and promising to make amends. Some time after their release Winston actually saw all three in the Chestnut Tree Café. He watched them from the corner of his eye with terrified fascination. They were much older, relics of the ancient heroic Party, still faintly glamorous from underground struggle and civil war. He has the blurry feeling he knew their names before Big Brother's. They were also outlaws, untouchables, certain to be destroyed within a year or two. No one who had once fallen into the hands...

## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| relevant | the one that really matters |
| exposed | publicly named as guilty |
| spectacular | staged to look huge and dramatic |
| incriminate | make themselves (or others) look guilty |
| intelligence (with the enemy) | secret contact / spying for the other side |
| embezzlement | stealing money you were trusted to look after |
| intrigues | secret plots |
| pardoned | officially forgiven |
| reinstated | put back into their old organisation |
| sinecures | jobs that pay or sound important but involve little real work |
| defection | leaving your side for the enemy |
| amends | something done to put a wrong right |
| relics | leftover pieces of an older world |
| glamour | a romantic shine of danger and fame |
| extinction | being wiped out completely |
