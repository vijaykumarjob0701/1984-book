## Summary

Surrender feels like turning round in a current you could never beat: nothing outside you changes, only your attitude. Anything can be true. If O'Brien thinks he floats, and Winston thinks he sees it, then it happens. A forbidden thought surfaces — it does not really happen; it is hallucination — and he pushes it under. The error, he tells himself, is believing in a "real" world outside minds. Still, the thought should never have arisen. The mind must grow a blind spot: crimestop. He drills it. The Party says the earth is flat; ice is heavier than water. He practises not seeing the arguments against those claims. It takes both sharp logic and the skill of going suddenly stupid.
