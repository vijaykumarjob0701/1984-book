## Summary

Those unnamed people also join the Two Minutes Hate. In the next cubicle the sandy-haired woman spends her days deleting from the press the names of the vaporized — people treated as never having lived. There is a grim fitness to this, because her own husband was vaporized two years ago. Nearby, mild, dreamy Ampleforth, hairy-eared and gifted with rhyme, produces "definitive" garbled versions of poems that have become ideologically offensive but must stay in anthologies. This hall of about fifty workers is only one cell of Records. Beyond it are swarms of other jobs: huge print shops, photo-faking studios, a tele-programmes section with actors who imitate voices, armies of clerks listing books to recall, vast stores of corrected files, and hidden furnaces. Somewhere, unnamed "directing brains" decide which fragment of the past to keep, fake, or erase. Records itself is only one branch of the Ministry of Truth, whose main job is not the past but supplying Oceania with newspapers, films, textbooks, programmes, plays, novels — every kind of information or entertainment, from a statue to a slogan, a lyric to a science treatise, a spelling-book to a Newspeak dictionary.
