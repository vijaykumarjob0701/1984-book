## Summary

DARK could be UNLIGHT, or LIGHT UNDARK — a matter of which root you keep. The second grammar mark is regularity. Almost all past forms end in *-ED* (STEALED, THINKED); irregulars such as SWAM are abolished. Plurals are *-S* or *-ES* even for MAN, OX, LIFE → MANS, OXES, LIFES. Comparison is always *-ER*, *-EST* (GOODER, GOODEST). Only pronouns, relatives, demonstratives, and helper-verbs stay irregular; WHOM goes, and SHALL/SHOULD yield to WILL/WOULD. Extra letters may be kept if a word is hard to say — especially in the B list — because ease of pronunciation will matter later. The B vocabulary is then introduced: words built on purpose for politics, each meant to push the user's mind into the right attitude. Without Ingsoc you can hardly use them correctly.
