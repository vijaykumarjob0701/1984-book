## Summary

...of poles, none thicker than a wrist. Nothing is big enough to hide a mike, and she has been here before. They are only making conversation. He has managed to move closer. She stands very upright with a faintly ironical smile, as if wondering why he is so slow. The bluebells have cascaded to the ground as if of their own accord. He takes her hand. Until this moment, he says, he did not know the colour of her eyes — brown, a rather light shade, with dark lashes. Now that she has seen what he is really like, can she still bear to look at him? Yes, easily. He is thirty-nine, has a wife he cannot get rid of, varicose veins, five false teeth. She could not care less. The next moment — hard to say by whose act — she is in his arms. At first he feels only sheer incredulity. The youthful body is against his, dark hair against his face; she has turned her face up and he is kissing the wide red mouth. She clasps his neck and calls him darling, precious, loved one. He pulls her down; she is utterly unresisting. The truth is he has almost no physical sensation except contact — only incredulity and pride. He is glad it is happening, but he has no desire yet. It is too soon; her youth and prettiness have frightened him; he is too used to living without women. She sits up, pulls a bluebell from her hair, and puts her arm round his waist. Never mind — no hurry — they have the whole afternoon. Isn't this a splendid hide-out? She found it when she got lost on a community hike. If anyone were coming you could hear them a hundred metres away. What is her name? Julia. She already knows his: Winston Smith.
