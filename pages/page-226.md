## Summary

...documentary evidence proving that their confessions were false. There was a certain photograph about which he had a hallucination. He believed that he had actually held it in his hands. It was a photograph something like this. An oblong slip of newspaper appears between O'Brien's fingers. For perhaps five seconds it is within the angle of Winston's vision. It is a photograph, and there is no question of its identity. It is *the* photograph. It is another copy of the photograph of Jones, Aaronson, and Rutherford at the party function in New York, which he chanced upon eleven years ago and promptly destroyed. For only an instant it is before his eyes, then it is out of sight again. But he has seen it, unquestionably he has seen it! He makes a desperate, agonizing effort to wrench the top half of his body free. It is impossible to move so much as a centimetre in any direction. For the moment he has even forgotten the dial. All he wants is to hold the photograph in his fingers again, or at least to see it. "It exists!" he cries. "No," says O'Brien. He steps across the room. There is a memory hole in the opposite wall. O'Brien lifts the grating. Unseen, the frail slip of paper is whirling away on the current of warm air; it is vanishing in a flash of flame. O'Brien turns away from the wall. "Ashes," he says. "Not even identifiable ashes. Dust. It does not exist. It never existed." Winston insists that it did exist, that it exists in memory, that they both remember it. "I do not remember it," says O'Brien. Winston's heart sinks. That was doublethink. He has a feeling of deadly helplessness. If he could have been certain that O'Brien was lying, it would not have seemed to matter. But it is perfectly possible that O'Brien has really forgotten the photograph. And if so, then already he would have forgotten his denial of remembering it, and forgotten the act of forgetting. How could one be sure that it was simple trickery? Perhaps that lunatic dislocation in the mind could really happen: that was the thought that defeated him.
