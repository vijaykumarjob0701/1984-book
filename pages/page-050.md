## Summary

...there will be nothing else. In the end, Syme says, goodness and badness will be covered by only six words — really one. He asks if Winston sees the beauty of that, then adds it was Big Brother's idea. A empty, eager look crosses Winston's face at the name, but Syme still spots his lack of real enthusiasm. Syme says Winston does not truly appreciate Newspeak: even when he writes it he thinks in Oldspeak. His *Times* pieces are decent translations, but in his heart he prefers Oldspeak's vague shades of meaning. Newspeak is the only language whose vocabulary shrinks every year. Winston knows this; he smiles and stays silent. Syme explains the aim: narrow the range of thought until thoughtcrime is literally impossible, because there will be no words for it. Each needed idea will have exactly one tightly defined word; extra meanings will be rubbed out. The Eleventh Edition is already close, and the process will go on after they are dead. Consciousness will keep shrinking. Even now thoughtcrime is only a failure of self-discipline and reality-control; later even that will be unnecessary. When the language is perfect, the Revolution is complete: Newspeak is Ingsoc. By 2050, he says, no living person will understand a conversation like this one. Winston starts to say "Except —" and stops.
