## Summary

The fright is not only that the figure is himself. Close up, the face is a jailbird's: bald scalp, crooked nose, seamed cheeks, fierce eyes. His body is grey with old dirt, scarred, the ankle ulcer inflamed. The horror is the thinness — ribs like a skeleton, knees thicker than thighs, spine bent into a cavity. He would have guessed a man of sixty with a killing disease. O'Brien spins him round and inventories the filth, the stink, the running sore, the lost twenty-five kilograms. He pinches the bicep until finger and thumb meet, says he could snap the neck like a carrot, and pulls out a handful of hair.
