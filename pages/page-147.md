## Summary

Chapter 7 opens with Winston waking in tears. Julia rolls sleepily against him, murmuring something that might be "What's the matter?" He begins "I dreamt —" and stops. It is too complex: there is the dream itself, and a memory that swam up in the few seconds after waking. He lies back, eyes shut, still sodden in the dream's atmosphere. It is a vast, luminous dream in which his whole life seems to stretch out like a landscape on a summer evening after rain. It all occurred inside the glass paperweight, but the surface of the glass was the dome of the sky, and inside everything was flooded with clear soft light in which one could see into interminable distances. The dream was also comprehended by — in some sense it consisted in — a gesture of the arm made by his mother, and made again thirty years later by the woman he had seen on the news film, trying to shelter the small boy from the bullets before the helicopter blew them both to pieces. He tells Julia that until this moment he believed he had murdered his mother. "Why did you murder her?" she asks, almost asleep. He did not murder her. Not physically. In the dream he remembered his last glimpse of his mother, and within a few moments of waking the cluster of small events around it had all come back — a memory he must have deliberately pushed out of consciousness over many years. He is not certain of the date, but he could not have been less than ten, possibly twelve. His father had disappeared some time earlier; he cannot remember how much earlier.
