## Summary

Part Two opens mid-morning. Winston has left his cubicle for the lavatory. The dark-haired girl comes toward him down the long bright corridor. Four days have passed since he met her outside the junk-shop. Her right arm is in a sling the colour of her overalls; she has probably crushed her hand on one of the Fiction Department's novel-plot kaleidoscopes, a common accident. When they are about four metres apart she stumbles and falls almost flat, crying out as she lands on the injured arm. Winston stops. She rises to her knees, face milky yellow, mouth very red, eyes fixed on him with a look more like fear than pain. A curious feeling stirs: she is an enemy trying to kill him, and also a human creature in pain, perhaps with a broken bone. He has already started forward; when she fell on the bandage he seemed to feel the pain himself. He asks if she is hurt. She says it is nothing, her heart fluttering, very pale. He asks if anything is broken; she says no, it only hurt for a moment. She holds out her free hand; he helps her up. Colour returns.
