## Summary

The man yells "Steamer!" — the prole nickname for a rocket bomb — and tells Winston to lie down. Winston throws himself on his face. Proles are nearly always right about these warnings; they seem to sense a rocket seconds before it arrives, though the weapons supposedly travel faster than sound. He clasps his arms over his head. A roar heaves the pavement; light objects patter on his back. When he stands he is covered in glass from the nearest window. Up the street the bomb has demolished a group of houses. A black plume of smoke hangs over plaster dust and a forming crowd. On the pavement ahead is a little pile of plaster with a bright red streak. It is a human hand, cut off at the wrist, so whitened it looks like a plaster cast. He kicks it into the gutter and turns down a side-street to avoid the crowd. In three or four minutes he is out of the blast area and sordid street life is going on as if nothing happened. It is nearly twenty hours. Prole drinking-shops — they call them pubs — are packed. From grimy swing doors comes a smell of urine, sawdust, and sour beer. In an angle of a house-front three men stand close, studying a folded newspaper. Even before Winston can see their faces he reads absorption in their bodies. It looks like serious news. A few paces on, the group breaks and two men are in violent argument, almost at blows.
