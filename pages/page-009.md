## Summary

In the alcove Winston is out of the telescreen's sight, though not its hearing. That odd room layout, plus the old book, has given him the idea for what he will do. The book's creamy, yellowed paper has not been made for decades; he found it in a dirty junk shop and felt a sudden need to own it. Party members are not supposed to use ordinary shops — "dealing on the free market" — but people still do, because even shoelaces and razor blades are hard to get otherwise. He slipped in, paid two dollars fifty, and took the book home in a briefcase. Even empty, it is a dangerous object. He is about to start a diary. Nothing is illegal because there are no laws, but being caught would likely mean death or twenty-five years in a labour camp. He fits a real nib to an old-fashioned pen, feeling the paper deserves it rather than an ink-pencil. People usually dictate into a speak-write, which he cannot use for this. He dips the pen, then hesitates as a tremor runs through him.
