## Summary

...it easily enough. Darts start again and the men at the bar talk lottery tickets; Winston is forgotten for a moment. He finds a deal table under the window where they can talk without being overheard. It is horribly dangerous, but there is no telescreen — he checked as soon as he came in. The old man still grumbles that a half-litre does not satisfy and a whole litre starts his bladder, let alone the price. Winston tries tentatively: the old man must have seen great changes. The pale blue eyes travel from the dartboard to the bar to the Gents, as if change would have happened inside this room. Finally he says the beer was better and cheaper: mild beer — wallop — was fourpence a pint, before the war. Which war? "It's all wars," he says vaguely, then drinks Winston's health. The beer vanishes down his lean throat. Winston fetches two more half-litres; the old man seems to forget his prejudice against a full litre. Winston presses: the man is much older and can remember the days before the Revolution. People of Winston's age only know those times from books, which may not be true. He wants an opinion. The history books say life then was completely different: terrible oppression, injustice, poverty worse than anything now. Here in London...
