## Summary

...and body seem afflicted with an unbearable sensitivity, a kind of transparency that makes every movement, sound, contact, and word an agony. Even in sleep he cannot escape her image. He does not touch the diary. If there is relief, it is work, where he can sometimes forget himself for ten minutes. He has no clue what happened to her and no enquiry he can make. She may have been vaporized, killed herself, or been transferred across Oceania; worst and likeliest, she simply changed her mind. The next day she reappears, arm out of the sling, sticking-plaster on her wrist. Relief is so great he cannot help staring for several seconds. The following day he very nearly speaks. She is alone at a table well out from the wall; the place is not full. The queue edges forward, then sticks for two minutes while someone ahead complains about missing saccharine. She is still alone when he gets his tray and walks casually toward her, eyes hunting a seat beyond. She is three metres away; another two seconds would do it. A voice behind calls "Smith!" He pretends not to hear; it calls louder. A blond, silly-faced young man named Wilsher, whom he barely knows, is smiling him into a vacant place. It is not safe to refuse. Once recognized he cannot sit with an unattended girl — too noticeable. He sits with a friendly smile. Wilsher beams. Winston has a hallucination of smashing a pick-axe into that face. The girl's table fills a few minutes later. But she must have seen him coming; perhaps she will take the hint. Next day he arrives early.
