## Summary

The man looks frantically round at the other prisoners, as though with some idea that he could put another victim in his own place. His eyes settle on the smashed face of the chinless man. He flings out a lean arm. That's the one they ought to be taking, not him! They didn't hear what he was saying after they bashed his face. Give him a chance and he'll tell them every word of it. *He's* the one that's against the Party, not me. The guards step forward. The man's voice rises to a shriek. They didn't hear him! Something went wrong with the telescreen. *He's* the one they want. Take him, not me! The two sturdy guards stoop to take him by the arms. But just at this moment he flings himself across the floor of the cell and grabs one of the iron legs that support the bench. He has set up a wordless howling, like an animal. The guards take hold of him to wrench him loose, but he clings on with astonishing strength. For perhaps twenty seconds they are hauling at him. The prisoners sit quiet, their hands crossed on their knees, looking straight in front of them. The howling stops; the man has no breath left for anything except hanging on. Then there is a different kind of cry. A kick from a guard's boot has broken the fingers of one of his hands. They drag him to his feet. "Room 101," says the officer. The man is led out, walking unsteadily, with head sunken, nursing his crushed hand; all the fight has gone out of him. A long time passes. If it had been midnight when the skull-faced man was taken away, it is morning; if morning, it is afternoon. Winston is alone, and has been alone for hours. The pain of sitting on the narrow bench is such that often he gets up and walks about, unreproved by the telescreen. The piece of bread still lies where the chinless man dropped it. At the beginning it needs a hard effort not to look at it, but presently hunger gives way to thirst. His mouth is sticky and evil-tasting. The humming sound and the unvarying white light induce a sort of faintness, an empty feeling inside his head. He will get up because the ache in his bones is no longer bearable, and then will sit down again almost at once because he is too dizzy to make sure of staying on his feet.
