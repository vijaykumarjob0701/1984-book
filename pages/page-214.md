## Summary

"Down with Big Brother!" Yes, he said that! Said it over and over again, it seems. Between you and me, old man, he is glad they got him before it went any further. Does Winston know what he is going to say when he goes up before the tribunal? "Thank you," he is going to say, "thank you for saving me before it was too late." Who denounced him? It was his little daughter, says Parsons with a sort of doleful pride. She listened at the keyhole. Heard what he was saying, and nipped off to the patrols the very next day. Pretty smart for a nipper of seven, eh? He doesn't bear her any grudge for it. In fact he is proud of her. It shows he brought her up in the right spirit, anyway. He makes a few more jerky movements up and down, several times casting a longing glance at the lavatory pan. Then he suddenly rips down his shorts. Excuse me, old man. He can't help it. It's the waiting. He plumps his large posterior into the lavatory pan. Winston covers his face with his hands. "Smith!" yells the voice from the telescreen. "6079 Smith W.! Uncover your face. No faces covered in the cells." Winston uncovers his face. Parsons uses the lavatory, loudly and abundantly. It then turns out that the plug is defective and the cell stinks abominably for hours afterwards. Parsons is removed. More prisoners come and go, mysteriously. One, a woman, is consigned to "Room 101," and, Winston notices, seems to shrivel and turn a different colour when she hears the words. A time comes when, if it had been morning when he was brought here, it would be afternoon; or if afternoon, then midnight. There are six prisoners in the cell, men and women. All sit very still. Opposite Winston sits a man with a chinless, toothy face exactly like that of some large, harmless rodent. His fat, mottled cheeks are so pouched at the bottom that it is difficult not to believe he has little stores of food tucked away there. His pale-grey eyes flit timorously from face to face and turn quickly away again when he catches anyone's eye.
