## Summary

...the thought that he might lose her, that the white youthful body might slip away. What he fears most is that she will change her mind if he does not reach her quickly. But the physical difficulty of meeting is enormous — like trying to move at chess when you are already mated. Every way you turn, the telescreen faces you. All possible ways of communicating occurred to him within five minutes of the note; now he lays them out one by one. This morning's accident cannot be repeated. If she worked in Records it might be simpler, but he barely knows where Fiction is and has no pretext to go there. If he knew her address and leaving time he could contrive to meet her on the way home, but following her would mean loitering outside the Ministry, which would be noticed. A letter is out of the question: all mail is opened as a routine that is not even secret. Few people write letters anyway; occasional messages use printed postcards where you strike out unused phrases. He does not know her name, let alone her address. The safest place, he decides, is the canteen: if he can get her alone at a mid-room table, not too near the screens, with enough buzz around them for thirty seconds, they might exchange a few words. For a week life is a restless dream. The next day she appears only as he is leaving, whistle already blown — probably a later shift. They pass without a glance. The day after she is there at the usual time, but with three girls, straight under a telescreen. Then for three dreadful days she does not appear at all. His whole mind...
