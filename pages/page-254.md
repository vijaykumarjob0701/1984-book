## Summary

Stupidity, he finds, is as necessary as intelligence and as hard to learn. One part of his mind still wonders when they will shoot him. O'Brien said everything depends on himself, but there is no act that brings the bullet closer. They might keep him years, send him to a camp, or even release him for a time; death comes from behind, in a corridor, without warning. Once he slips into a blissful waking dream: the bullet is about to come, everything is settled, he walks in sunlight into the Golden Country — turf, elms, the stream with dace under the willows. Then horror: he has cried aloud, "Julia! Julia! Julia, my love! Julia!" For a moment she seemed not only with him but inside him.
