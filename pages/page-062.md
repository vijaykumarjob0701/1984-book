## Summary

...the sexual act. Not love so much as erotic feeling is the enemy, in marriage as well as outside it. Party marriages need a committee's approval, and permission is always refused if the couple seem physically attracted, though that rule is never clearly stated. The only recognised purpose of marriage is to produce children for the Party. Sex is to be treated as a slightly disgusting minor operation. That is never said plainly, but it is rubbed in from childhood. Groups like the Junior Anti-Sex League call for complete celibacy. In theory all children would be conceived by artificial insemination (artsem in Newspeak) and raised in institutions. Winston knows this is not meant entirely seriously, but it fits Party ideology. The Party wants to kill the sex instinct, or else twist and dirty it. He does not know why, but it feels natural that they would. With women, the effort has largely worked. He thinks of Katharine. They parted nine, ten, nearly eleven years ago; he can forget for days that he was ever married. They lived together only about fifteen months. The Party forbids divorce but encourages separation when there are no children. Katharine was tall, fair, straight, splendid in movement, with a bold aquiline face that looked noble until you found almost nothing behind it. Very early he decided she had the emptiest, most slogan-filled mind he had ever known. There was no official stupidity she could not swallow.
