## Summary

The instructions always speak of slips, errors, misprints, or misquotations to put right for accuracy. Adjusting Plenty's figures, Winston thinks it is not even forgery: one piece of nonsense replaces another. Most of the material has no link to the real world, not even the link a direct lie has. Statistics are fantasy before and after "correction"; often you invent them. Plenty had forecast 145 million pairs of boots; "actual" output is 62 million; he marks the forecast down to 57 million so the quota can still be called overfulfilled. None of those numbers is likelier than the others. Probably no boots were made, or nobody knows or cares. Paper produces astronomical boot figures while half of Oceania may go barefoot. Every recorded fact fades into a shadow-world where even the year is unsure. Across the hall, precise, dark-chinned Tillotson works with a folded paper on his knee, mouth close to the speakwrite, as if keeping a secret with the screen. His glasses flash hostility when he looks up. Winston barely knows him or his job. People in Records do not talk about work. In the windowless hall he sees a dozen unnamed colleagues daily, hurrying or gesticulating.
