## Summary

...feeling any compulsion to get up, simply lying there and listening to peaceful sounds outside. Surely there could never have been a time when that seemed ordinary? Julia wakes, rubs her eyes, and raises herself on her elbow to look at the oilstove. Half that water has boiled away; she'll make coffee in another moment. They have an hour. What time do they cut the lights off at his flats? Twenty-three thirty. Twenty-three at the hostel — but you have to get in earlier, because — Hi! Get out, you filthy brute! She suddenly twists over, seizes a shoe, and sends it hurtling into the corner with a boyish jerk, exactly as he saw her fling the dictionary at Goldstein during the Two Minutes Hate. What was it? A rat. She saw its beastly nose out of the wainscoting; there's a hole down there. She gave him a good fright, anyway. Rats! he murmurs. In this room! They're all over the place, she says indifferently; even in the hostel kitchen. Some parts of London are swarming with them. Did he know they attack children? Yes. In some streets a woman daren't leave a baby alone two minutes. It's the great huge brown ones. And the nasty thing is that the brutes always — "DON'T GO ON!" Winston says, eyes tightly shut. Dearest! He's gone quite pale. Do they make him feel sick? Of all horrors in the world — a rat! She presses herself against him and winds her limbs round him to reassure him with warmth. He does not reopen his eyes at once. For several moments he has the feeling of being back in a nightmare that has recurred throughout his life. It is always much the same. He is standing in front of a wall of darkness, and on the other side is something unendurable, too dreadful to be faced. In the dream his deepest feeling is always self-deception, because he does in fact know what...
