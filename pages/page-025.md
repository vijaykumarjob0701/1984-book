## Summary

That outward rage targets enemies of the State, foreigners, traitors, saboteurs, and thought-criminals. People over thirty are often afraid of their own children, with reason: almost every week *The Times* praises a "child hero" who overheard a parent and reported them. The catapult sting fades. Winston picks up the pen without energy, then thinks of O'Brien. Seven years ago he dreamed of walking through a pitch-dark room. A quiet voice to one side said they would meet in the place where there is no darkness — a statement, not an order. At the time it meant little; later it grew important. He cannot remember whether he had already met O'Brien, but he now identifies the voice as O'Brien's. Even after this morning's look he cannot tell if O'Brien is friend or enemy, and it hardly seems to matter. What matters is a link of understanding stronger than liking or political sides. He does not know what the sentence means, only that it will come true somehow. The telescreen pauses; a clear trumpet sounds; then a rasping voice announces a newsflash from the Malabar front and a victory in South India.
