## Summary

She has no memories from before the early sixties. The only person she knew who talked often of pre-Revolution days was a grandfather who disappeared when she was eight. At school she was hockey captain and won the gymnastics trophy two years running. She was a Spies troop-leader and a Youth League branch secretary before the Junior Anti-Sex League. She has always borne an excellent character. She was even picked — an infallible mark of good reputation — to work in Pornosec, the Fiction sub-section that turns out cheap pornography for the proles. Workers call it Muck House. She stayed a year, helping produce sealed booklets with titles like spanking stories, bought furtively by prole youths who thought they were buying something illegal. What are the books like? Ghastly rubbish, she says, boring, only six plots swapped round. She was only on the kaleidoscopes, never Rewrite; she is not literary. Winston learns with astonishment that all Pornosec workers except department heads are girls. The theory is that men's sex instincts are less controllable, so they are in greater danger of being corrupted by the filth. They do not even like having married women there. Girls are always supposed to be so pure. Here is one who isn't. She had her first love-affair at sixteen with a Party member of sixty who later killed himself to avoid arrest. A good job too, she says — otherwise they would have had her name out of him when he confessed. There have been various others since. Life as she sees it is quite simple.
