## Summary

Chapter 4 opens in a slightly kinder cell. Winston is fatter and stronger, if one can still speak of days. The white light and hum are the same, but there is a pillow, a mattress, a stool, baths, warm water, new clothes, ointment on the ulcer, and dentures in place of the last teeth. Weeks or months have passed; meals come at regular intervals, sometimes with meat, once with cigarettes that the silent guard will light. They give him a white slate and a stub of pencil. At first he is too torpid to use it. He sleeps easily under the glare. The dreams are happy: the Golden Country, sunlit ruins, sitting with his mother, Julia, and O'Brien talking of peaceful things. Awake, he has almost lost the power of hard thought.
