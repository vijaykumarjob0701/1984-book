## Summary

The street swarms: girls in full bloom with crude lipstick, youths chasing them, swollen waddling women who show what the girls will become, bent old people on splayed feet, ragged barefoot children who play in puddles and scatter when mothers yell. About a quarter of the windows are broken and boarded. Most people ignore Winston; a few watch him with guarded curiosity. Two huge women with brick-red forearms talk in a doorway. He catches scraps: one says it is easy to criticize if you have not had her problems; the other agrees. Their sharp voices stop. They study him in a silence that is not quite hostility — more a wary stiffening, as if an unfamiliar animal were passing. Party blue overalls are rare here. It is unwise to be seen in such streets without definite business. Patrols might stop you and ask for papers, why you are here, when you left work, whether this is your usual way home. There is no rule against an unusual route, but it is enough to draw Thought Police attention. Suddenly the whole street is in commotion. People yell warnings and dive into doorways like rabbits. Ahead, a young woman snatches a child from a puddle, wraps it in her apron, and leaps back inside in one movement. A man in a concertina-like black suit runs from an alley toward Winston, pointing at the sky.
