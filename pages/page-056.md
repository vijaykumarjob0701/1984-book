## Summary

The telescreen even mentions demonstrations thanking Big Brother for raising the chocolate ration to twenty grams. Only yesterday it was announced that the ration would be reduced to twenty. Can people swallow that in twenty-four hours? Yes. Parsons swallows it with animal stupidity. The eyeless speaker swallows it fanatically and would hunt anyone who remembered last week's thirty grams. Syme swallows it too, in a more complex doublethink way. Winston wonders if he alone still has a memory. Fabulous statistics pour out: more food, clothes, houses, furniture, fuel, ships, books, babies — more of everything except disease, crime, and insanity. Everyone and everything is "whizzing rapidly upwards." Winston dabbles in pale gravy, resenting the physical texture of life. Has food always tasted like this? The canteen is low, crowded, grimy from countless bodies; tables are battered and jammed together; spoons bent, trays dented, mugs coarse; everything greasy, with a sour mix of bad gin, bad coffee, metallic stew, and dirty clothes. In stomach and skin there is a protest, a sense of being cheated. Yet he has no clear memory of anything much better. In any time he can accurately remember there was never quite enough to eat, socks and underwear were full of holes, furniture was rickety, rooms cold, trains packed, houses falling...
