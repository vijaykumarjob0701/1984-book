## Summary

Chapter 5 opens with Syme gone. One morning he is simply not at work. A few careless people mention the absence; the next day nobody does. On the third day Winston checks the Records Department notice-board. The Chess Committee list looks almost unchanged — nothing is crossed out — but it is one name shorter. That is enough: Syme has ceased to exist; he has never existed. Outside it is baking hot; inside the maze of windowless Ministry rooms the air stays cool. Hate Week preparations are in full swing and every Ministry is on overtime: processions, parades, lectures, waxworks, films, stands, effigies, slogans, songs, rumours, and faked photographs. Julia's Fiction unit has been pulled off novels to rush out atrocity pamphlets. Winston, on top of his usual work, spends long hours rewriting back files of *The Times* so speakers will have the "right" quotations. At night rowdy prole crowds give the town a feverish feel. Rocket bombs fall more often, and distant unexplained explosions breed wild rumours. The new Hate Song — a savage barking rhythm more like a drum than music — is plugged endlessly on the telescreens. Roared out to marching feet it is terrifying. The proles like it; in the midnight streets it competes with the old song "It was only a hopeless fancy." The Parsons...
