## Summary

On a bright, cold April day, clocks strike thirteen as Winston Smith hurries into Victory Mansions, trying to keep out a dirty wind. The hall smells of boiled cabbage and old mats, and a huge poster of a moustached man stares from the wall. The lift is off as part of an electricity-saving drive before Hate Week, so the thirty-nine-year-old Winston, slowed by a varicose ulcer, climbs seven flights past more posters whose eyes seem to follow him. The caption says Big Brother is watching. Inside his flat a voice from a wall plaque — the telescreen — reads pig-iron production figures; he can turn it down but never fully off. He is a thin Party member in blue overalls. Outside, the street looks colourless and cold.
