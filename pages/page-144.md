## Summary

Chapter 6 opens: it has happened at last. The expected message has come. All his life, it seems, he has been waiting for this. He is walking down the long Ministry corridor, almost at the spot where Julia slipped him the note, when he becomes aware that someone larger is just behind him. The person gives a small cough, as a prelude to speaking. Winston stops and turns. It is O'Brien. Face to face at last, his only impulse is to run. His heart bounds violently; he would be incapable of speaking. O'Brien, however, continues forward in the same movement, laying a friendly hand on Winston's arm so they walk side by side. He speaks with the peculiar grave courtesy that sets him apart from most Inner Party members. He has been hoping for a chance to talk. He was reading one of Winston's Newspeak articles in *The Times*. Winston takes a scholarly interest in Newspeak, he believes? Winston recovers part of his self-possession. Hardly scholarly: he is only an amateur; it is not his subject; he has never had anything to do with the actual construction of the language. O'Brien says he writes it very elegantly — not only O'Brien's opinion. He was talking recently to a friend of Winston's who is certainly an expert. The name has slipped his memory for the moment. Winston's heart stirs painfully. It is inconceivable that this is anything other than a reference to Syme. But Syme is not only dead: he is abolished, an unperson. Any identifiable mention of him would be mortally dangerous. O'Brien's remark must have been intended as a signal, a codeword.
