## Summary

Here we reach the central secret. As we have seen, the mystique of the Party, and above all of the Inner Party, depends upon doublethink. But deeper than this lies the original motive, the never-questioned instinct that first led to the seizure of power and brought doublethink, the Thought Police, continuous warfare, and all the other necessary paraphernalia into existence afterwards. This motive really consists… The sentence breaks off. Winston becomes aware of silence, as one becomes aware of a new sound. It seems to him that Julia has been very still for some time. She is lying on her side, naked from the waist upwards, cheek pillowed on her hand, one dark lock tumbling across her eyes. Her breast rises and falls slowly and regularly. He says her name. No answer. "Julia, are you awake?" No answer. She is asleep. He shuts the book, puts it carefully on the floor, lies down, and pulls the coverlet over both of them. He has still, he reflects, not learned the ultimate secret. He understood *how*; he did not understand *why*. Chapter I, like Chapter III, has not actually told him anything that he did not know; it has merely systematized the knowledge he already possessed. But after reading it he knows better than before that he is not mad. Being in a minority, even a minority of one, did not make you mad. There was truth and there was untruth, and if you clung to the truth even against the whole world, you were not mad. A yellow beam from the sinking sun slants in through the window and falls across the pillow. He shuts his eyes. The sun on his face and the girl's smooth body touching his own give him a strong, sleepy, confident feeling. He is safe; everything is all right. He falls asleep murmuring "Sanity is not statistical," with the feeling that this remark contains a profound wisdom. A row of asterisks marks a break. Chapter 9 ends here.
