## Summary

...though she had a map inside her head. Can he remember all that? Yes. Left, then right, then left again, and the gate has no top bar. What time? About fifteen; he may have to wait; she will come another way. Is he sure? Yes. Then get away from her as quick as he can. She need not have said it, but they cannot yet extricate themselves. The trucks still file past; people still gape insatiably. At first there were a few boos and hisses, only from Party members, soon stopped. The main feeling is simple curiosity. Foreigners, Eurasian or Eastasian, are a kind of strange animal. You never see them except as prisoners, and even then only for a moment. Nobody knows what becomes of them, apart from the few hanged as war-criminals; the rest vanish, presumably into labour camps. The round faces give way to more European ones, dirty, bearded, exhausted. From over scrubby cheekbones eyes look into Winston's, sometimes with strange intensity, then flash away. The convoy is ending. In the last truck an aged man stands, face a mass of grizzled hair, wrists crossed as if used to being bound. It is almost time to part. At the last moment, while the crowd still hems them in, her hand finds his and gives it a fleeting squeeze. It cannot have been ten seconds, yet it seems long that their hands are clasped. He has time to learn every detail of her hand: long fingers, shapely nails, a work-hardened palm with a row of callouses, smooth flesh under the wrist. From feel alone he would know it by sight.
