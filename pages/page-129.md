## Summary

...again, by a conscious, wilful act, one chose to shorten the interval before it happened. Quick steps on the stairs: Julia bursts in carrying a coarse brown canvas tool-bag like those at the Ministry. He starts forward to take her in his arms, but she disengages rather hurriedly, still holding the bag. Half a second — let her show what she has brought. Did he bring that filthy Victory Coffee? She thought he would. He can chuck it away; they will not need it. Look here. She falls on her knees, throws the bag open, and tumbles out spanners and a screwdriver that filled the top. Underneath are neat paper packets. The first she passes him has a strange yet vaguely familiar feel: heavy, sand-like stuff that yields wherever you touch it. It isn't sugar? Real sugar. Not saccharine — sugar. And a loaf of proper white bread, not their bloody stuff, and a little pot of jam, and a tin of milk — but look, this is the one she is really proud of. She had to wrap a bit of sacking round it, because — She need not tell him. The smell is already filling the room, a rich hot smell like an emanation from his early childhood, the kind you still occasionally meet blowing down a passage before a door slams, or diffusing itself in a crowded street, sniffed for an instant and then lost. It's coffee, he murmurs — real coffee. Inner Party coffee. A whole kilo. How did she get hold of all these things? It's all Inner Party stuff. There's nothing those swine don't have. But waiters and servants pinch things, and — look, she got a little packet of tea as well. He squats beside her and tears a corner. It's real tea. Not blackberry leaves.
