## Summary

Does he exist in the same way as I exist? "You do not exist," says O'Brien. Once again the sense of helplessness assails him. He knows, or he can imagine, the arguments which proved his own nonexistence; but they were nonsense, they were only a play on words. Did not the statement "You do not exist" contain a logical absurdity? But what use was it to say so? His mind shrivels as he thinks of the unanswerable, mad arguments with which O'Brien would demolish him. "I think I exist," he says wearily. He is conscious of his own identity. He was born and he shall die. He has arms and legs. He occupies a particular point in space. No other solid object can occupy the same point simultaneously. In that sense, does Big Brother exist? It is of no importance. He exists. Will Big Brother ever die? Of course not. How could he die? Next question. Does the Brotherhood exist? That, Winston, you will never know. If we choose to set you free when we have finished with you, and if you live to be ninety years old, still you will never learn whether the answer to that question is Yes or No. As long as you live it will be an unsolved riddle in your mind. Winston lies silent. His breast rises and falls a little faster. He still has not asked the question that had come into his mind the first. He has got to ask it, and yet it is as though his tongue will not utter it. There is a trace of amusement in O'Brien's face. Even his spectacles seem to wear an ironical gleam. He knows, thought Winston suddenly, he knows what I am going to ask! At the thought the words burst out of him: "What is in Room 101?" The expression on O'Brien's face does not change. He answers drily that Winston already knows what is in Room 101 — everyone knows. He raises a finger to the man in the white coat. Evidently the session is at an end. A needle jerks into Winston's arm. He sinks almost instantly into deep sleep. Chapter 2 ends there.

## Difficult phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| "You do not exist" | O'Brien denies Winston even as a person occupying space |
| "He exists" | Big Brother's existence is not the same kind of fact as a body in a room |
| "you will never know" | whether the Brotherhood is real is to stay an unsolved riddle |
| "What is in Room 101?" | the question he has been afraid to ask |
| "Everyone knows what is in Room 101" | O'Brien refuses a description; the answer is treated as already known |
