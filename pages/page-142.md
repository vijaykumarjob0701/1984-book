## Summary

...story of Jones, Aaronson, and Rutherford and the momentous slip of paper he once held. It makes little impression. At first she fails to grasp the point. Were they friends of his? No: Inner Party men, older, from before the Revolution; he barely knew them by sight. Then what was there to worry about? People are being killed off all the time. He tries to make her understand: this was not just somebody being killed. The past, starting from yesterday, has been abolished. If it survives anywhere, it is in a few solid objects with no words attached — like that lump of glass. They already know almost nothing about the Revolution and the years before it. Every record has been destroyed or falsified, every book rewritten, every picture repainted, every statue and street and building renamed, every date altered, and the process continues minute by minute. History has stopped. Nothing exists except an endless present in which the Party is always right. He knows the past is falsified, but he could never prove it, even when he did the falsification himself. After the thing is done, no evidence remains. The only evidence is inside his mind, and he cannot be sure anyone else shares his memories. Only that once, years after the event, he held actual concrete evidence. And what good was that? No good, because he threw it away a few minutes later. But if the same thing happened today, he would keep it. Julia would not. She will take risks, but only for something worthwhile, not bits of old newspaper. What could he have done with it even if he had kept it? Not much, perhaps. But it was evidence. It might have planted a few doubts, if he had dared show it. He does not imagine they can alter anything in their own...

## Difficult vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| momentous | hugely important |
| impression | a strong effect on the mind |
| abolished | wiped out so it no longer counts as real |
| falsified | changed so the record is no longer true |
| concrete | solid and physical, not just a memory |
| worthwhile | important enough to justify the risk |

## Difficult phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| "the past, starting from yesterday, has been actually abolished" | even recent history is being erased as if it never happened |
| "solid objects with no words attached" | things like the paperweight still exist, but they cannot tell a story |
| "History has stopped" | there is no true past left, only the Party's present |
| "an endless present" | a now in which the Party is always already right |
| "planted a few doubts" | showing the photo might have made a few people stop and wonder |
