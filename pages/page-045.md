## Summary

Big Brother's style is both military and pedantic, easy to copy because he asks a question and answers it at once. Winston invents Ogilvy's life: at three the child wants only a drum, a sub-machine gun, and a model helicopter; at six he joins the Spies a year early; at nine he is a troop leader; at eleven he denounces his uncle after overhearing "criminal" talk; at seventeen he organises the Junior Anti-Sex League; at nineteen he designs a grenade that kills thirty-one Eurasian prisoners; at twenty-three he dies. Chased over the Indian Ocean, he weights himself with a machine gun and leaps from a helicopter with his despatches — an end Big Brother says one can only envy. Ogilvy is described as a total abstainer, nonsmoker, gym-only, sworn to celibacy because a family would clash with round-the-clock duty. He talks only of Ingsoc and lives to defeat Eurasia and hunt spies. Winston considers giving him the Order of Conspicuous Merit, then decides against it to avoid extra cross-references. He glances at Tillotson, sure they share the job, and feels a deep conviction that his own version will be chosen. Comrade Ogilvy, unimagined an hour ago...
