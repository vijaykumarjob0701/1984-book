## Summary

He turns toward Julia and adds, with somewhat more expression, that she must understand: even if Winston survives, it may be as a different person. They may be obliged to give him a new identity. His face, his movements, the shape of his hands, the colour of his hair — even his voice would be different. And she herself might have become a different person. Their surgeons can alter people beyond recognition. Sometimes it is necessary. Sometimes they even amputate a limb. Winston cannot help snatching another sidelong glance at Martin's face. There are no scars that he can see. Julia has turned a shade paler, so that her freckles are showing, but she faces O'Brien boldly. She murmurs something that seems to be assent. Good. Then that is settled. There is a silver box of cigarettes on the table. With a rather absent-minded air O'Brien pushes them toward the others, takes one himself, then stands up and begins to pace slowly to and fro, as though he can think better standing. They are very good cigarettes, very thick and well-packed, with an unfamiliar silkiness in the paper. He looks at his wrist-watch again. Martin had better go back to his pantry. O'Brien will switch on in a quarter of an hour. Take a good look at these comrades' faces before you go. You will be seeing them again. I may not. Exactly as at the front door, the little man's dark eyes flicker over their faces. There is not a trace of friendliness. He is memorizing their appearance, but he feels no interest in them, or appears to feel none. It occurs to Winston that a synthetic face is perhaps incapable of changing its expression. Without speaking or giving any kind of salutation, Martin goes out, closing the door silently. O'Brien strolls up and down, one hand in the pocket of his black overalls, the other holding his cigarette. They will understand, he says, that they will be fighting in the dark.
