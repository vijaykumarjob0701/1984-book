## Summary

...somehow to stay alive until the spasm was over. He has ceased to notice whether he is crying out or not. The pain lessens again. He opens his eyes. O'Brien has drawn back the lever. How many fingers, Winston? Four. He supposes there are four. He would see five if he could. He is trying to see five. Which does he wish: to persuade O'Brien that he sees five, or really to see them? Really to see them. "Again," says O'Brien. Perhaps the needle is eighty — ninety. Winston cannot intermittently remember why the pain is happening. Behind his screwed-up eyelids a forest of fingers seems to be moving in a sort of dance, weaving in and out, disappearing behind one another and reappearing again. He is trying to count them, he cannot remember why. He knows only that it is impossible to count them, and that this is somehow due to the mysterious identity between five and four. The pain dies down again. When he opens his eyes it is to find that he is still seeing the same thing. Innumerable fingers, like moving trees, are still streaming past in either direction, crossing and recrossing. He shuts his eyes again. How many fingers am I holding up, Winston? I don't know. I don't know. You will kill me if you do that again. Four, five, six — in all honesty I don't know. "Better," says O'Brien. A needle slides into Winston's arm. Almost in the same instant a blissful, healing warmth spreads all through his body. The pain is already half-forgotten. He opens his eyes and looks up gratefully at O'Brien. At sight of the heavy, lined face, so ugly and so intelligent, his heart seems to turn over. If he could have moved he would have stretched out a hand and laid it on O'Brien's arm. He has never loved him so deeply as at this moment, and not merely because he has stopped the pain. The old feeling, that at bottom it did not matter whether O'Brien was a friend or an enemy, has come back. O'Brien was a person who could be talked to.
