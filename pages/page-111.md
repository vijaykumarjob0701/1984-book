## Summary

How did she find his name? She expects she is better at finding things out than he is. What did he think of her before the note? He feels no temptation to lie; telling the worst is even a kind of love-offering. He hated the sight of her. He wanted to rape her and then murder her. Two weeks ago he seriously thought of smashing her head with a cobblestone. If she really wants to know, he imagined she had something to do with the Thought Police. She laughs delightedly, taking this as a tribute to her disguise. Not the Thought Police — he didn't honestly think that? Well, perhaps not exactly, but from her general appearance — young, fresh, healthy — he thought... She finishes: he thought she was a good Party member, pure in word and deed, banners, processions, slogans, games, hikes, and that given a quarter of a chance she would denounce him and get him killed. Yes, something of that kind; a great many young girls are like that. "It's this bloody thing that does it," she says, ripping off the Junior Anti-Sex League sash and flinging it on a bough. Then, as if her waist reminded her, she pulls a small slab of chocolate from her overalls, breaks it, and gives him half. Even before he takes it the smell tells him it is unusual: dark, shiny, silver-wrapped. Ordinary chocolate is dull-brown crumbly stuff that tastes like a rubbish-fire. He has tasted chocolate like this before. The first whiff stirs a powerful, troubling memory he cannot pin down. Where did she get this stuff?
