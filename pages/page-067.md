## Summary

...have grabbed the same saucepan and are tearing at it until the handle comes off. Winston watches in disgust. For a moment that cry from a few hundred throats had sounded almost frighteningly powerful. Why can they never shout like that about anything that matters? He writes that until they become conscious they will never rebel, and until they have rebelled they cannot become conscious — a line that might have come from a Party textbook. The Party claims it freed the proles from capitalist bondage: starvation, floggings, women in coal mines (they still work there), children sold into factories at six. At the same time, by doublethink, it teaches that proles are natural inferiors who must be kept down like animals. In reality little is known about them, and little needs to be known. So long as they work and breed, the rest does not matter. Left alone, they have gone back to an old pattern: born in gutters, work at twelve, a short bloom of youth and desire, marry at twenty, middle-aged at thirty, dead around sixty. Hard work, home, petty fights, films, football, beer, and above all gambling fill their minds. Control is easy. A few Thought Police agents spread rumours and remove the rare person who looks dangerous, but no attempt is made...
