## Summary

Sure enough she is alone at about the same table. Ahead in the queue is a small, swift, beetle-like man with a flat face and tiny suspicious eyes. As Winston turns from the counter the little man makes straight for her table. Hopes sink. There is an emptier table further on, and the man looks comfort-loving enough to take it. With ice at his heart Winston follows; it is no use unless he can get her alone. Then a tremendous crash: the little man is on all fours, tray flying, soup and coffee streaming across the floor. He starts up with a malignant glance, clearly thinking Winston tripped him. But it is all right. Five seconds later, heart thundering, Winston is at the girl's table. He does not look at her. He unpacks and starts eating. He must speak at once, before anyone else comes, but a terrible fear has him: a week has passed; she must have changed her mind; such things do not happen in real life. He might have flinched from speaking if he had not seen Ampleforth, the hairy-eared poet, wandering with a tray. Ampleforth is vaguely attached to Winston and will sit with him if he sees him. There is perhaps a minute. Both eat steadily — a thin stew of haricot beans, really a soup. In a low murmur, neither looking up, they spoon the watery stuff and exchange the few necessary words in expressionless voices. What time does she leave work? Eighteen-thirty. Where can they meet? Victory Square, near the monument.
