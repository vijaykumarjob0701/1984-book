Even now his intellect says the message probably means death, but that is not what he believes. Unreasonable hope lasts; his heart bangs; he can hardly keep his voice from trembling as he murmurs figures into the speakwrite. He sends the finished work down the tube. Eight minutes have gone. He sighs, draws the next batch over with the scrap on top, and flattens it. In large unformed handwriting it says: I LOVE YOU. For several seconds he is too stunned even to throw the incriminating thing into the memory hole. When he does, he cannot resist reading it once more to be sure the words are there. The rest of the morning is hard to work. Worse than the niggling jobs is hiding his agitation from the telescreen. A fire seems to burn in his belly. Lunch in the hot, noisy canteen is torment. He had hoped to be alone; instead Parsons flops down, sweat almost beating the tinny stew-smell, and talks nonstop about Hate Week — especially a two-metre papier-mâché Big Brother head his daughter's Spies troop is making.
