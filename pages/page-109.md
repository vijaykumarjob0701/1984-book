## Summary

...when a crackle of a foot on twigs freezes him. He keeps picking bluebells. It is the best thing to do: it may be the girl, or he may have been followed. To look round is to show guilt. A hand falls lightly on his shoulder. It is the girl. She shakes her head — keep silent — then parts the bushes and leads him along the narrow track. She has been this way before and dodges the boggy bits by habit. He follows, still clasping the flowers. First comes relief; then, watching the strong slender body and the tight scarlet sash, a heavy sense of his own inferiority. Even now she may draw back when she looks at him. The sweetness of the air and greenness of the leaves daunt him. Already the May sun has made him feel dirty and etiolated, an indoor creature with London soot in his pores. Till now she has probably never seen him in open daylight. They reach the fallen tree. She hops over, forces the bushes, and they are in a natural clearing: a tiny grassy knoll shut in by tall saplings. She stops and turns. "Here we are." He faces her at several paces and does not yet dare come nearer. She did not want to speak in the lane in case a mike is hidden; she does not suppose there is one, but one of those swine might recognise a voice. They are all right here. He still has not the courage to approach. "We're all right here?" he repeats stupidly. Yes — look at the trees. They are small ashes once cut down and sprouted again into a forest...
