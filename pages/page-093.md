## Summary

...had been coming back at all after buying the diary, without knowing whether the owner could be trusted. Still — he will come back. He will buy further scraps of beautiful rubbish, take the St Clement Danes engraving from its frame, carry it home under his overalls, and drag the rest of the poem out of Mr Charrington. Even the mad idea of renting the upstairs room flashes again. For perhaps five seconds exaltation makes him careless. He steps onto the pavement without a glance through the window, even humming an improvised tune of oranges and lemons. Suddenly his heart turns to ice and his bowels to water. A figure in blue overalls is coming down the pavement, not ten metres away: the dark-haired girl from the Fiction Department. The light is failing, but there is no difficulty recognising her. She looks him straight in the face, then walks quickly on as if she had not seen him. For a few seconds he is too paralysed to move. Then he turns right and walks heavily away, not noticing at first that it is the wrong direction. One question is settled: she is spying on him. She must have followed him; it is not credible that by pure chance she should walk the same obscure backstreet, kilometres from any Party quarter, on the same evening. The coincidence is too great. Whether she is Thought Police or an amateur spy driven by officiousness hardly matters. She is watching him. She has probably seen him go into the pub as well. Walking is an effort. The lump of glass bangs his thigh at each step; he is half minded to throw it away. The worst thing is the pain in his belly.
