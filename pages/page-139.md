## Summary

...pinchbeck locket holding a strand of a long-dead baby's hair. Charrington never asks Winston to buy; he only wants him to admire. Talking to him is like listening to a worn-out musical-box. He has dragged more forgotten rhyme-fragments from memory: four and twenty blackbirds; a cow with a crumpled horn; the death of poor Cock Robin. "It just occurred to me you might be interested," he says with a deprecating little laugh. He can never recall more than a few lines of any one rhyme. Both lovers know, and never quite forget, that this cannot last. Sometimes impending death feels as solid as the bed, and they cling together in despairing sensuality, like a damned soul grabbing a last pleasure with the clock about to strike. Sometimes they have the illusion of safety and even permanence. Getting there is dangerous, but the room itself is sanctuary — like gazing into the paperweight and feeling that once inside that glassy world, time could be stopped. They daydream of escape: luck holding for the rest of their lives; Katharine dying so they can manoeuvre a marriage; suicide together; disappearing into prole accents and factory jobs on a back-street. They both know it is nonsense. There is no escape. Even the one practical plan, suicide, they do not mean to carry out. Hanging on from day to day, spinning out a present with no future, feels like an unconquerable instinct — as lungs keep drawing the next breath while there is air.
