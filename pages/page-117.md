## Summary

Chapter 3 opens with Julia saying they can use this hide-out once more — generally safe twice, but not for another month or two. As soon as she woke, her manner changed. She becomes alert and business-like, dresses, knots the scarlet sash, and arranges the journey home. It seems natural to leave this to her. She has a practical cunning Winston lacks and an exhaustive knowledge of the countryside around London, stored from countless community hikes. The route she gives him is quite different from the way he came and brings him out at another station. Never go home the same way you went out, she says, as if stating a general principle. She will leave first; he is to wait half an hour. She names a meeting place four evenings later: a street in a poorer quarter with a crowded open market. She will hang about the stalls, pretending to look for shoelaces or thread. If the coast is clear she will blow her nose when he approaches; otherwise he must walk past without recognition. With luck, in the middle of the crowd, they can talk a quarter of an hour and arrange another meeting. Now she must go: due back at nineteen-thirty, two hours of Junior Anti-Sex League leaflet work. Isn't it bloody? She asks for a brush-down — any twigs in her hair? Then goodbye, my love. She flings herself into his arms, kisses him almost violently, and a moment later slips through the saplings and vanishes with very little noise. He still has not found out her surname or address.
