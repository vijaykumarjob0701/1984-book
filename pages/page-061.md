## Summary

He writes that he went with her through a backyard into a basement kitchen: a bed against the wall, a lamp turned very low. His teeth are on edge; he wants to spit. At the same time he thinks of Katharine, his wife. He is — or was — married; as far as he knows she is not dead. He seems to smell the kitchen again: bugs, dirty clothes, and cheap scent that is still alluring because no Party woman uses scent. Only proles do, and in his mind the smell is mixed with sex. Going with that woman was his first lapse in about two years. Visiting prostitutes is forbidden, but it is a rule you can sometimes nerve yourself to break. Dangerous, not usually life-and-death: five years in a labour camp if you are caught in the act and have no worse crime. Poor quarters swarm with women who will sell themselves, some for a bottle of gin the proles are not supposed to drink. Quietly the Party even leans toward encouraging prostitution as an outlet for instincts it cannot fully crush. Joyless, furtive debauchery with a despised class does not matter much. The unforgivable crime is promiscuity between Party members — though purge victims always confess to it, he can barely imagine it happening.
