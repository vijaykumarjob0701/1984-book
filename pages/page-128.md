## Summary

"It's all off," she murmurs when it is safe — tomorrow afternoon. She cannot come. Why not? The usual reason; it has started early this time. For a moment he is violently angry. In the month he has known her the nature of his desire has changed. At the beginning there was little true sensuality; the first time was an act of the will. After the second time it was different. The smell of her hair, the taste of her mouth, the feeling of her skin seem to have got inside him. She has become a physical necessity, something he not only wants but feels he has a right to. When she cannot come he feels she is cheating him. Just then the crowd presses them together and their hands accidentally meet. She gives his fingertips a quick squeeze that seems to invite not desire but affection. It strikes him that when one lived with a woman this particular disappointment must be a normal, recurring event, and a deep tenderness he has not felt before suddenly takes hold. He wishes they were a married couple of ten years' standing, walking these streets openly, talking of trivialities, buying odds and ends for the household. Above all he wishes they had some place to be alone without the obligation to make love every time they met. Not actually at that moment, but sometime the next day, the idea of renting Charrington's room occurs to him. When he suggests it she agrees with unexpected readiness. Both know it is lunacy. It is as though they were intentionally stepping nearer to their graves. Sitting on the edge of the bed waiting, he thinks again of the Ministry of Love cellars. It is curious how that predestined horror moves in and out of consciousness. There it lies, fixed in future time, preceding death as surely as 99 precedes 100.
