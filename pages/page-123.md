## Summary

...become in effect an extension of the Thought Police: a device by which everyone is surrounded night and day by informers who know him intimately. Abruptly his mind goes back to Katharine. She would unquestionably have denounced him if she had not been too stupid to detect his unorthodoxy. What really recalls her now is the stifling afternoon heat bringing sweat out on his forehead. He tells Julia of something that happened — or rather failed to happen — on another sweltering summer afternoon eleven years ago. They had been married three or four months. They lost their way on a community hike in Kent. They lagged only a couple of minutes, took a wrong turning, and found themselves pulled up short by an old chalk quarry: a sheer drop of ten or twenty metres, boulders at the bottom. There was nobody to ask. As soon as Katharine realised they were lost she became very uneasy. To be away from the noisy mob of hikers even for a moment felt like wrong-doing. She wanted to hurry back and search the other way. Then Winston noticed tufts of loosestrife growing in cracks of the cliff, one of two colours — magenta and brick-red — apparently on the same root. He had never seen anything like it and called her to look. She had already turned to go, but she rather fretfully came back and even leaned out over the cliff to see. He stood a little behind her and put his hand on her waist to steady her. At that moment it suddenly occurred to him how completely alone they were. Not a human creature anywhere, not a leaf stirring, not even a bird awake.
