## Summary

Chapter 2 finds Winston walking up a wooded lane through dappled light, stepping into pools of gold where the boughs part. Bluebells mist the ground; the air seems to kiss the skin. It is the second of May. Deeper in the wood, ring-doves drone. He is a bit early. The journey was easy, and the girl is so clearly experienced that he is less frightened than usual. He trusts her to find a safe place. The country is not much safer than London: no telescreens, but hidden microphones might pick up a recognised voice, and travelling alone draws attention. Under 100 kilometres you need no extra passport stamp, but station patrols sometimes question Party members. None appeared, and on the walk from the station he checked he was not followed. The train was full of holiday proles. His wooden-seated carriage overflowed with one enormous family, from a toothless great-grandmother to a month-old baby, going to country in-laws and, they freely tell him, to get a little black-market butter. The lane widens to the cattle-track she described. He has no watch; it cannot be fifteen yet. Bluebells are so thick he cannot help treading on them. He kneels and picks a bunch, partly to pass time, partly with a vague wish to offer her flowers. He has a big bunch and is smelling their faint sickly scent...
