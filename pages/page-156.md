## Summary

...might have been the beginnings of a smile. With his characteristic gesture O'Brien resettles his spectacles. "Shall I say it, or will you?" Winston says he will say it. Is that thing really turned off? Yes: everything is turned off. They are alone. "We have come here because ——" He pauses, realising for the first time the vagueness of his own motives. Since he does not in fact know what kind of help he expected, it is not easy to say why he has come. He goes on, conscious that he must sound both feeble and pretentious. They believe there is some kind of conspiracy, some kind of secret organisation working against the Party, and that O'Brien is involved in it. They want to join it and work for it. They are enemies of the Party. They disbelieve in the principles of Ingsoc. They are thought-criminals. They are also adulterers. He tells this because they want to put themselves at O'Brien's mercy. If O'Brien wants them to incriminate themselves in any other way, they are ready. He stops and glances over his shoulder, feeling that the door has opened. Sure enough, the little yellow-faced servant has come in without knocking, carrying a tray with a decanter and glasses. "Martin is one of us," says O'Brien impassively. Bring the drinks over; put them on the round table. Have they enough chairs? Then they may as well sit down and talk in comfort. Bring a chair for yourself, Martin. This is business. You can stop being a servant for the next ten minutes. The little man sits down, quite at his ease, and yet still with a servant-like air — the air of a valet enjoying a privilege. Winston regards him out of the corner of his eye. It strikes him that the man's whole life is playing a part, and that he feels it dangerous to drop his assumed personality even for a moment. O'Brien takes the decanter by the neck and fills the glasses with a dark-red liquid. It arouses in Winston dim memories of something seen long ago on a wall or a hoarding — a vast bottle of electric lights that seemed to move up and down and pour its contents into a glass.
