## Summary

He cannot clearly remember a time without war, but childhood must have had a long peace, because an air raid once surprised everyone — perhaps when an atomic bomb hit Colchester. He does not recall the raid itself, only his father's hand as they hurried down a ringing spiral staircase until his legs failed and he whimpered. His mother followed slowly with the baby — or maybe only blankets; he is unsure his sister was born. They come out into a crowded Tube station. People sit on the stone floor and on stacked metal bunks. Nearby an old couple sit together. The man wears a decent dark suit and a black cap pushed back from white hair; his face is scarlet, his blue eyes full of tears. He smells of gin so strongly the tears seem made of it. He is a little drunk, but his grief is real and unbearable. Child-Winston senses something unforgivable has happened — perhaps a granddaughter killed. Every few minutes the old man repeats that they should not have trusted "them," that he said so all along. Winston can no longer remember which people he meant.
