## Summary

In his mind he nicknamed her "the human sound-track." He could have endured living with her except for sex. When he touched her she winced and stiffened; embracing her was like holding a jointed wooden image. Even while clasping him she seemed to be pushing him away. She would lie with shut eyes, neither resisting nor helping, only submitting. It was embarrassing, then horrible. He could still have stayed if they had agreed to remain celibate, but she refused: they must produce a child if they could. The act happened once a week when possible. She even reminded him in the morning. She had two names for it — "making a baby" and "our duty to the Party." He grew to dread the appointed day. No child came; she gave up; they parted. Winston sighs without a sound and writes again: the street woman threw herself on the bed and, with no kindness or preface, pulled up her skirt in the coarsest way. He sees himself in dim lamplight, smelling bugs and cheap scent, feeling defeat and resentment mixed with the thought of Katharine's body, frozen by the Party's hypnotic power.
