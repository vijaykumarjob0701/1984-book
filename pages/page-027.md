## Summary

The watching eyes and surrounding voice are everywhere — asleep or awake, working or eating, indoors or out, in the bath or in bed. Nothing is his except the few cubic centimetres inside his skull. Sunlight has left the Ministry's many windows, which now look like grim fortress slits. His heart shrinks before the pyramid: it is too strong to storm, even with a thousand rocket bombs. He wonders again who the diary is for — future or past, perhaps an imaginary age. Ahead lies not ordinary death but annihilation: the book will be ash, he will be vapour, and only the Thought Police will read him before wiping him from memory. How can you appeal to the future if no physical trace can survive? The telescreen strikes fourteen; he must leave in ten minutes to be at work by fourteen-thirty. Oddly, the hour gives him heart. He is a lonely ghost speaking a truth nobody will hear, but speaking it keeps some human thread unbroken. Staying sane, not being heard, is how you carry the heritage. He writes a greeting to a time when thought is free, people are not identical or alone, and truth cannot be undone — from the age of uniformity, solitude, Big Brother, and doublethink.
