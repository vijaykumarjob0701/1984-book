## Summary

The past is changeable by nature, yet it is treated as if it had never been changed. Whatever is true now is treated as true forever. All you need is endless victories over your own memory. They call this reality control; in Newspeak, doublethink. The instructress barks "Stand easy" a little more kindly. Winston drops his arms and breathes. His mind slides into doublethink's maze: to know and not know; to feel truthful while telling built lies; to hold two cancelling opinions and believe both; to use logic against logic; to reject morality while claiming it; to call democracy impossible and the Party its guardian; to forget, remember when needed, then forget again — and to do the same to that process itself. The last trick is to make yourself unconscious on purpose, then forget you hypnotised yourself. Even understanding the word uses doublethink. She calls them to attention for toe-touching. Winston hates the exercise: shooting pains from heels to buttocks, often another cough. Pleasant thought dies. He decides the past has not only been altered but destroyed. How can you prove the obvious if nothing exists outside your memory? He tries to recall when he first heard of Big Brother — perhaps the 1960s, but he cannot be sure. Party histories make Big Brother the Revolution's leader from the start, his deeds pushed further and further back.
