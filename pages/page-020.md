## Summary

He does not tear the pages out. Writing the slogan or not, keeping the diary or not, no longer matters: the Thought Police will take him anyway. He has already committed the core crime that includes all others — thoughtcrime — which cannot stay hidden forever. Arrests come at night: a jerk from sleep, a hand on the shoulder, lights, hard faces. Usually there is no trial and no news. People vanish; their names and records are erased; their past existence is denied. The usual word is vaporized. Panic hits and he scrawls that they will shoot him in the back of the neck and that he does not care, mixed with more "down with big brother." He stops, a little ashamed, and puts the pen down. Then he jumps: someone is knocking. He freezes, hoping they will leave, but the knock comes again. Delay would be worst. His heart pounds, but habit keeps his face blank. He gets up and walks heavily to the door.
