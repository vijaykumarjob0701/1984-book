## Summary

Chapter 2 opens as Winston reaches the door-knob and sees he has left the diary open, the huge letters readable across the room. That was stupid, but even in panic he did not want to smudge the wet ink. He opens the door and feels relief: it is Mrs Parsons, a colourless, worn neighbour of about thirty who looks older, with dust seeming to sit in the lines of her face. She calls him "comrade" in a tired, whining voice and asks him to look at a blocked kitchen sink because Tom is not home. The Party prefers "comrade" to "Mrs," but people still say Mrs by habit. Winston follows her. Victory Mansions (built around 1930) is falling apart — flaking plaster, burst pipes, leaks, half-dead heating — and official repairs can wait years. The Parsons flat is larger and dingier, trampled and littered with sports kit and dirty dishes.
